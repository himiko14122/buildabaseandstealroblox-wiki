## The Official Discord Server

The single most reliable source of new Build a Base and Steal codes is the **official Discord server**. Developers post codes in the dedicated **Codes channel** in real time, often within seconds of the code being pushed live. The Discord is also where the developers first announce upcoming updates, and most major updates are accompanied by at least one new code as a launch gift.

To get the most out of the Discord, complete a few setup steps after joining. First, link your account by linking it to your Roblox profile. Verification is free and takes less than a minute. Without verification, you may miss codes that are tied to linked-account rewards. Second, enable notifications for the Codes channel. Discord lets you mute individual channels while leaving notifications on for the ones you care about, and the Codes channel should be at the top of your notification list. Third, opt into the announcements role so you receive push notifications on your phone when a new code is posted.

Beyond real-time drops, the Discord also archives every past code in a pinned post. This archive is useful when you want to verify whether an old code is still active or to catch up on rewards you missed. The pinned post is updated by the developers, but our wiki provides a more user-friendly archive that filters by expiration date and reward type.

### The Codes Channel Etiquette

The Codes channel is a busy space with hundreds of messages per day, especially during events. To find new codes quickly, use Discord's "Jump to Present" feature or scroll past the conversation chatter until you see messages from the developer bot. The bot posts codes with a distinctive template, usually starting with the word "CODE" and including the reward description. Memorizing this template helps you spot real codes faster, even in a noisy channel.

Avoid posting fake codes in the channel. Posting a fake code triggers confusion and wastes other players' time, and the moderators will issue warnings or bans for repeat offenders. If you are unsure whether a code is real, post your question in the help channel rather than the Codes channel, and a moderator will clarify.

## The Official X / Twitter Account

The second-fastest source of new codes is the **official X account**, which posts codes both as standalone tweets and as part of larger update announcements. The X account has the advantage of being publicly visible, meaning you do not need to join any server to see new posts, and the platform's algorithm makes it easy to find older codes via the search function.

The drawback of X is that posts are easily buried. The X algorithm favors recency and engagement, so an old code post may not appear in your feed even if you follow the account. To prevent this, enable "All Tweets" notifications for the official account rather than just "Top Tweets." The "All Tweets" setting ensures you see every post, not just the ones the algorithm thinks you will engage with.

X is also where livestream codes appear most frequently. During developer livestreams, new codes are sometimes dropped exclusively in chat and are never posted to Discord or anywhere else. If you only enable "Posts," you will miss livestream drops. Enable "Posts and replies" plus "Live videos" notifications to capture every channel through which the developers might distribute codes.

### The X Search Trick

If you missed a code and want to find it retroactively, use X's advanced search with the query "from:[official handle] code" and a date range. This will surface every tweet that contains the word "code" from the official account within your specified window. The advanced search is faster than scrolling the timeline, especially for accounts that post frequently.

## Avoiding Fake Codes

The fake-code ecosystem around Build a Base and Steal is more active than most players realize. Clickbait websites post fake codes to drive ad revenue, shady YouTube channels post fake codes to inflate view counts, and bot accounts post fake codes in the comment sections of legitimate creators' videos. Learning to identify fakes is essential to avoid wasting time.

Common red flags for fake codes include codes that are unusually long (real codes are typically 6 to 12 characters), codes that require you to "like and subscribe" before working (real codes work for every player), codes that ask you to visit a third-party site to "unlock" the reward (real codes work directly in-game), and codes that promise rewards that are obviously too good to be true (no real code grants 50,000 cash or 100 free rolls).

If a code fails to redeem, do not spend time looking for variations. The code is either fake, expired, or mistyped. Move on to the next entry on the active list rather than chasing a code that does not exist. Time spent on fake codes is time stolen from your actual play session, and disciplined code hunters know when to cut their losses.
