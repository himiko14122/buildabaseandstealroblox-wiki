Pet evolution and potions are the mid-to-late game progression systems in Build a Base and Steal. Rolling for high-rarity pets is the primary way to grow your roster, but evolution lets you upgrade existing pets and potions let you boost their stats beyond their natural limits. The systems are complex, the costs are significant, and not every evolution or potion use is optimal. This guide breaks down the mechanics in detail and helps you make smart decisions about where to invest your limited resources.

## How Pet Evolution Works

Pet evolution in Build a Base and Steal is a one-way transformation that takes a pet from a lower rarity to a higher rarity, at the cost of evolution materials and cash. The transformation preserves the pet's appearance, its special ability, and its friendship level, but increases its base rate and unlocks higher stat caps. A common pet evolved to uncommon gains a roughly 50% increase in base rate. Uncommon to rare adds another 50%. Rare to epic adds another 50%. Epic to legendary adds another 50%. Legendary to mythic adds another 50%.

The cumulative effect is significant. A common pet evolved all the way to mythic gains a roughly 7.5x increase in base rate, which makes evolution a viable alternative to rolling for new pets. However, the evolution cost scales dramatically with each tier, and the materials required for the final evolution to mythic are rare and expensive. Most players will only evolve a handful of pets to mythic over the course of their career, because the material cost is too high to do it repeatedly.

### Evolution Materials and Where to Find Them

Evolution materials in Build a Base and Steal are split into two categories: common materials and rare materials. Common materials include evolution dust, shards, and crystals, which drop from rolling, from raids, and from daily quests. Rare materials include evolution essence, cores, and stars, which drop from high-level raids, from events, and from boss encounters. The rare materials are the bottleneck in the evolution system, because they drop at a fraction of the rate of common materials.

The most reliable source of rare materials is the boss encounter system. Bosses appear in Build a Base and Steal servers periodically, and defeating a boss guarantees a drop of rare materials. The exact material depends on the boss: fire bosses drop fire-type essence, water bosses drop water-type essence, and so on. The boss encounters are also the only source of evolution stars, which are required for the final evolution to mythic. Players who want to evolve pets should prioritize boss encounters whenever they appear.

### The Evolution Decision Tree

Not every pet is worth evolving. The decision tree for evolution starts with the pet's rarity and its friendship level. A pet at friendship level 1 should never be evolved, because you are not yet invested in that pet. A pet at friendship level 5 or higher is a strong candidate for evolution, because the time you have spent leveling its friendship represents an investment that evolution preserves. A pet at a low rarity (common or uncommon) is generally not worth evolving past epic, because the cost is high relative to the stat gain. A pet at a high rarity (epic or legendary) is worth evolving all the way to mythic, because the marginal cost of the final evolution is reasonable relative to the stat gain.

The second consideration is the pet's special ability. Some abilities scale better with rarity than others. An ability that provides a flat bonus, like "+10 cash per second," becomes relatively weaker as the pet's base rate increases. An ability that provides a percentage bonus, like "+10% income to adjacent pets," becomes relatively stronger as the pet's base rate increases. Pets with percentage-based abilities are better candidates for evolution, because the evolution multiplies the base rate, which in turn multiplies the effect of the ability.

## Rarity Potions: What They Do and When to Use Them

Rarity potions in Build a Base and Steal are consumable items that instantly evolve a pet from one rarity to the next, at the cost of the potion and no other materials. Rarity potions are extremely rare, and they are typically only available from events, from the premium shop, or from boss encounters. There are five types of rarity potions: uncommon potions, rare potions, epic potions, legendary potions, and mythic potions, corresponding to the five rarity tiers above common.

The advantage of a rarity potion is that it skips the material cost and the evolution cooldown, which can be significant for the final evolution to mythic. The disadvantage is that potions are scarce, and using a potion on the wrong pet is an irreversible mistake. The optimal use of rarity potions is on pets that are already at friendship level 5+ and that have percentage-based special abilities. These pets benefit the most from the rarity increase, and using a potion on them maximizes the value of the scarce potion resource.

### Potions vs. Traditional Evolution

The choice between a rarity potion and traditional evolution depends on your material stockpile and your timeline. If you have a surplus of rare materials and no immediate use for them, traditional evolution is the better choice, because it conserves potions for pets that you cannot afford to evolve with materials. If you are material-starved but you have potions saved from previous events, using a potion is the better choice, because it lets you upgrade a key pet without waiting for materials to drop.

The general rule is: evolve from common to uncommon, uncommon to rare, and rare to epic using materials. Evolve from epic to legendary using materials if you can afford it, or use a legendary potion if you cannot. Evolve from legendary to mythic using a mythic potion if you have one, or using materials if you do not. The final evolution to mythic is the most material-intensive step, and using a potion there is the highest-leverage use of potions, because it skips the most expensive material tier.

## Which Evolutions Are Worth It

The Build a Base and Steal community has reached a rough consensus on which evolutions are worth the investment. At the top of the list are the S-tier pets from the current meta: the Eternal Phoenix, the Quantum Leviathan, and the Cosmic Dragon. Evolving these pets to mythic is almost always worth it, because they are already the strongest pets in the game, and evolution pushes them even further ahead of the competition. The evolution cost is high, but the payoff in income is higher.

At the bottom of the list are B-tier pets and below. Evolving a B-tier pet to mythic is generally not worth it, because the mythic form of a B-tier pet is still weaker than the legendary form of an S-tier pet. The material cost would be better spent on evolving an S-tier pet instead. The exception is when the B-tier pet has a special ability that synergizes perfectly with your base configuration, and the evolution would unlock a stronger version of that ability. In that specific context, the evolution can be correct.

### The Early Game Exception

There is an important exception to the general rule about B-tier evolutions. In the early game, when your roster is small and every pet contributes meaningfully to income, evolving a B-tier pet to epic or legendary can be correct, even if that pet would be outclassed in the late game. The early game is about building a functional roster as quickly as possible, and evolving a B-tier pet is often faster than rolling for an A-tier or S-tier replacement.

The cutoff point is around friendship level 7 and 25-30 total pets in your roster. Before that point, B-tier evolutions are often correct. After that point, B-tier evolutions are rarely correct, because the roster is large enough that you can afford to be selective, and the material cost of evolving a B-tier pet would be better spent on evolving an A-tier or S-tier pet instead.

## Evolution and the Offline Income Multiplier

Evolution interacts directly with the offline income system in Build a Base and Steal, because offline income is calculated based on the pet's current base rate. A pet evolved from epic to legendary gains a roughly 50% increase in base rate, which translates directly into a 50% increase in that pet's contribution to offline income. For a pet that contributes 10% of your roster's total income, that evolution is a 5% increase in your total offline income, which is meaningful over a 24-hour accumulation window.

The cumulative effect of multiple evolutions is even more significant. If you evolve five pets from epic to legendary, and each contributes 10% of your total income, the total increase is 25% in offline income. Over a month of accumulation, that is a massive amount of additional cash. Evolution is not just about increasing your active income; it is also about increasing your passive income, and the passive gains compound over time.

### Timing Evolutions Around Events

The optimal time to evolve is during a rolling event, because the increased drop rate for pets makes it easier to acquire evolution materials. Boss encounters also spawn more frequently during events, which increases the supply of rare materials. Evolving during an event lets you complete the evolution faster and with less material grinding than evolving during a normal period.

The exception is for the final evolution to mythic. The mythic evolution should be timed around the offline cap. Evolve a legendary to mythic right before you log off for a 16-20 hour absence, and the mythic form will generate income for the entire accumulation window. Evolving right before you log in is suboptimal, because the pet spends most of its time at its previous rarity during the accumulation period.

## The Evolution Endgame

The evolution endgame in Build a Base and Steal is the state where you have evolved all of your key pets to mythic, and your roster consists entirely of mythic pets with maxed friendship levels and percentage-based special abilities. At this point, the evolution system is effectively complete, and further progression comes from refining your base configuration rather than from evolving new pets.

Reaching the evolution endgame takes months of consistent play, because the material cost for evolving 10+ pets to mythic is enormous. Most players will never fully complete the evolution endgame. Instead, they will evolve the 3-5 most important pets to mythic and leave the rest at legendary or below, because the marginal benefit of additional evolutions is not worth the material cost. The evolution system is designed to be a long-term goal, not a short-term grind, and the players who reach the endgame are the ones who play consistently over years rather than over weeks.

Evolution and potions are the depth of the Build a Base and Steal pet system. Rolling is the width: you collect many pets, but you only invest deeply in a few. Evolution is how you turn a casual collection into a specialized, optimized roster. Use it selectively, use it strategically, and use it to enhance the pets that are already the strongest in your configuration.
