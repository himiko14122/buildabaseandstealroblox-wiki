## How the Meta Shifts

The meta in Build a Base and Steal shifts for three reasons. First, new pets are released with stats that are intentionally competitive with existing top-tier pets. Second, balance patches adjust the rates of older pets, sometimes buffing pets that have fallen out of favor and sometimes nerfing pets that have become too dominant. Third, the player base discovers new synergies and combos that make previously overlooked pets valuable in specific contexts.

A pet that was S-tier three months ago may have dropped to A-tier or B-tier today, not because the pet itself changed, but because newer alternatives have higher base rates and similar utility. Conversely, a pet that was B-tier last month may have risen to A-tier after a balance patch buffed its rate by 10-15%. The tier list is not static, and chasing the previous patch's meta is a sure way to waste your rolling budget on pets that no longer perform.

### The Three Meta Tiers

In the current Build a Base and Steal meta, pets are divided into three tiers based on their effectiveness relative to the best available options. S-tier pets are the best in the game; they have the highest rates, the best utility, and no meaningful weaknesses. A-tier pets are strong but not top-tier; they are competitive with S-tier pets in specific contexts but are outclassed overall. B-tier pets are viable but not optimal; they have use cases in the early game or in specific roster configurations, but they are not worth chasing in the late game.

The cutoff between tiers is not hard. An S-tier pet in the current meta typically generates 20-30% more income than an A-tier pet of the same rarity, after accounting for aura and happiness bonuses. An A-tier pet typically generates 20-30% more income than a B-tier pet. The gap narrows in the early game, when every pet contributes meaningfully to income, but it widens sharply in the late game, when pet slots are tight and every slot needs to generate maximum value.

## S-Tier Pets (Current Meta)

### Eternal Phoenix
The Eternal Phoenix is the current king of the Build a Base and Steal pet meta. Released in the most recent update, it has a base rate of 950 per second at mythic rarity, which is the highest in the game. Its utility is also strong: it provides a 10% aura bonus to all fire-type pets within a 3-tile radius, and it has a special ability that occasionally boosts the income of adjacent pets by 20% for 30 seconds. The aura and the special ability stack, making the Eternal Phoenix the centerpiece of any meta-optimized roster.

The drawback is that the Eternal Phoenix is extremely rare. The base rate of 950 at mythic rarity is theoretical for most players, because the mythic form drops at a rate of roughly 0.01% in standard rolls. The legendary form drops at a rate of 0.1% and has a base rate of 650, which is still competitive with top-tier legendaries from previous patches. Players who roll heavily during Phoenix-themed events are most likely to obtain one, and even then it may take several events before a mythic appears.

### Quantum Leviathan
The Quantum Leviathan is the runner-up in the current meta. Its base rate at mythic rarity is 900 per second, which is slightly lower than the Eternal Phoenix, but its utility is unique: it provides a 15% aura bonus to all water-type pets, and its special ability periodically doubles the income of one random pet in the base for 60 seconds. The aura bonus is strong in water-themed bases, but the special ability is where the Leviathan shines, because the double effect can trigger on mythics and produce a massive short-term income spike.

The Leviathan is slightly more common than the Phoenix, with a mythic drop rate of roughly 0.02% in standard rolls and a legendary drop rate of 0.15%. The trade-off is that the Leviathan's special ability is random, so it is less consistent than the Phoenix's targeted adjacency bonus. For players who prefer consistency over upside, the Phoenix is the better choice. For players who enjoy the variance and want the possibility of a massive random bonus, the Leviathan is the better choice.

### Cosmic Dragon
The Cosmic Dragon is the third S-tier pet in the current meta, and it fills a different niche than the Phoenix or Leviathan. Its base rate at mythic rarity is 850 per second, which is lower than the top two, but its utility is defensive: it provides a 20% bonus to all trap damage within a 4-tile radius, and its special ability makes adjacent pets immune to theft for 60 seconds. This makes the Cosmic Dragon invaluable for base defense, especially in PvP-focused servers where raids are frequent.

The Dragon's drop rate is comparable to the Leviathan, with a mythic rate of roughly 0.02% and a legendary rate of 0.15%. The defensive utility is valuable in the right context, but for players in PvE-focused servers who rarely face raids, the Dragon's defensive bonuses are wasted, and the lower base rate makes it a weaker choice than the Phoenix or Leviathan. The Dragon is a contextual S-tier: top-tier in PvP servers, mid-tier in PvE servers.

## A-Tier Pets (Current Meta)

### Void Tiger
The Void Tiger is the leading A-tier pet in the current meta. Its base rate at mythic rarity is 750 per second, which is significantly lower than the S-tier options, but its utility is strong: it provides a 5% aura bonus to all dark-type pets, and its special ability reduces the cost of rolling by 10% for 120 seconds. The aura bonus is situational, but the rolling cost reduction is valuable for any player who rolls frequently, because it directly accelerates pet acquisition.

The Tiger's drop rate is higher than the S-tier pets, with a mythic rate of roughly 0.05% and a legendary rate of 0.25%. The drop rate advantage is meaningful for players who roll consistently but do not roll heavily during events. For these players, the Tiger is more accessible than the Phoenix or Leviathan, and the rolling cost reduction can eventually produce more pets over the long run.

### Solar Eagle
The Solar Eagle is another strong A-tier option. Its base rate at mythic rarity is 725 per second, and its utility is focused on happiness: it provides a 10% bonus to happiness for all light-type pets, and its special ability boosts the happiness of adjacent pets by 20% for 90 seconds. The happiness bonuses are valuable because happiness is a multiplicative income modifier, and the Eagle can indirectly increase the income of an entire roster by keeping happiness high.

The Eagle's drop rate is comparable to the Tiger, with a mythic rate of 0.05% and a legendary rate of 0.25%. The happiness utility is valuable for players who do not have the time to manually feed and rotate pets daily. For these players, the Eagle reduces the maintenance burden and keeps income high without active work. For players who already maintain high happiness manually, the Eagle's utility is less valuable, and the lower base rate makes it a weaker choice than the Tiger.

### Frost Serpent
The Frost Serpent is a specialized A-tier pet. Its base rate at mythic rarity is 700 per second, which is the lowest among A-tier options, but its utility is unique: it provides a 15% aura bonus to all ice-type pets, and its special ability freezes all raiders within a 5-tile radius for 30 seconds. The aura bonus is strong in ice-themed bases, but the freezing ability is where the Serpent shines, because it directly stops raids and gives the player time to prepare a defense or to call for reinforcements.

The Serpent's drop rate is comparable to the Eagle and Tiger, with a mythic rate of 0.05% and a legendary rate of 0.25%. The raid-utility is valuable in PvP servers, but in PvE servers the ability is wasted. For players who face frequent raids, the Serpent is worth pursuing despite the lower base rate. For players who rarely face raids, the Serpent is a weak choice, and a higher-rate A-tier pet like the Void Tiger or Solar Eagle would be better.

## B-Tier Pets (Current Meta)

### Spirit Wolf
The Spirit Wolf is the leading B-tier pet in the current meta. Its base rate at mythic rarity is 550 per second, which is significantly lower than A-tier options, but its utility is decent: it provides a 5% aura bonus to all spirit-type pets, and its special ability increases the rate of all pets in the base by 5% for 60 seconds. The aura bonus is weak, but the global rate increase is valuable in the early game, when every percentage point matters.

The Wolf's drop rate is higher than A-tier pets, with a mythic rate of 0.1% and a legendary rate of 0.5%. The drop rate advantage makes the Wolf accessible to new players, and the global rate increase can accelerate early-game progression. However, as players progress and acquire higher-rate pets, the Wolf's lower base rate becomes a liability, and it should be replaced by an A-tier or S-tier pet as soon as possible.

### Stone Golem
The Stone Golem is a tanky B-tier pet. Its base rate at mythic rarity is 500 per second, which is low even for B-tier, but its utility is defensive: it provides a 10% bonus to wall durability within a 3-tile radius, and its special ability increases the durability of all walls in the base by 20% for 120 seconds. The defensive bonuses are valuable for players who prioritize base defense over income, but for most players, the low base rate makes the Golem a weak choice.

The Golem's drop rate is comparable to the Wolf, with a mythic rate of 0.1% and a legendary rate of 0.5%. The defensive utility is valuable in PvP servers or for players who have been repeatedly raided, but for the average player, the Golem is not worth the pet slot. A higher-rate B-tier pet like the Spirit Wolf would produce more income, and income can eventually be spent on better walls directly.

## How to Chase the Meta

The optimal strategy for chasing the Build a Base and Steal meta is to focus your rolling budget on S-tier pets during their respective themed events, and to settle for A-tier pets when no S-tier events are active. The event bonus dramatically increases the drop rate for the featured pets, and rolling during an event is the most efficient way to obtain a mythic. For players who roll heavily, the difference in drop rate between an event and a standard roll can be as much as 10x, so timing your rolls around events is critical.

For players who cannot roll heavily, the meta is less relevant. A new player with a limited budget should focus on building a diverse roster of A-tier and B-tier pets, rather than chasing a single S-tier pet that may take months to appear. The cumulative income from a broad roster of moderate-rate pets will often exceed the income from a single S-tier pet until that S-tier pet reaches mythic rarity and is fully upgraded with auras and happiness bonuses.
