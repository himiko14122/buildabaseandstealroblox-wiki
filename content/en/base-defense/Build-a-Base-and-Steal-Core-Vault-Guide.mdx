The core vault is the innermost room in a serious Build a Base and Steal base, the section where your legendary, mythical, and otherwise irreplaceable pets live. Everything else in your base is either a buffer, a decoy, or a learning layer. The core vault is what you are actually defending. A player with a well-designed core vault can survive multiple coordinated raids. A player without one can the long term.

## What a Core Vault Is For

A core vault is not a room. It is a defensive philosophy that says the most valuable things in your base deserve the most protection, and the cost of providing that protection is justified by the value of what is being protected. The simplest bases have no vault at all, with all pets in a single open area and only an outer wall between them and any raider. The most serious bases have vaults with reinforced alloy walls, layered trap networks, hidden access, and decoy rooms designed to make the vault invisible.

The reason a vault matters is concentration of value. Legendary and mythical pets generate so much income and carry so much prestige that losing one is a setback measured in days of play, not hours. A base without a vault treats every pet the same, which means a raid that breaches the outer wall has access to everything. A base with a vault treats the legendary pets as a different category from the common pets, which means a raid that breaches the outer wall finds mostly decoys and is forced to either accept small loot or spend a great deal of time searching for the real valuables.

## Choosing the Vault Location

The location of the core vault is the most important defensive decision you from the entrance. It should be hidden in a way that the raider cannot identify the vault simply by looking at the base. And it should be accessible to you in one or two steps, not five or six, because you will be visiting it frequently.

### Center Placement vs. Edge Placement

Edge placement is intuitive but wrong. Putting the vault at the back of the base, against the far wall, means there is exactly one path to it: straight through the base. Raiders who identify that path will walk directly to the vault. Center placement is counterintuitive but more effective. Putting the vault in the geometric center of the base, surrounded by walls on all sides, means there are no obvious paths. Raiders must search the entire base to find the vault, and searching costs time and resources.

Center placement also makes traps more effective. A trap that is four tiles from the entrance is more likely to trigger than a trap that is one tile from the entrance, because the raider is more committed by the time they reach it. Place your strongest traps around the center, and you create a defensive ring that the raider must break through to reach the vault.

### Hiding the Vault in Plain Sight

The most common technique for hiding the vault is the decoy room. You build two rooms that look identical: a decoy room near the entrance with cheap that suggests it is the valuable area. Raiders who raid quickly will hit the decoy, take the cheap pets, and leave, never realizing the real vault exists.

A more advanced technique is the rotation vault. You move the location of the real vault every few days, so a raider who studied your base last week is now looking in the wrong place. Rotation is labor-intensive, but it is the most effective deterrent against repeated targeted raids. Pair rotation with a decoy that is fixed in the same location, and the decoy becomes a stable target that raiders hit repeatedly while the real vault shifts underneath them.

## Vault Construction

The materials and design of the vault are different from the rest of the base. The vault walls should be reinforced alloy or the highest tier you can afford, because the vault is the section you will never voluntarily replace. The vault traps should be the most expensive you own, because the vault is where they pay for themselves. The vault entrance should be a single door or a single wall section, because multiple entrances create multiple breach points and complicate your own access.

### Single-Entrance Discipline

A vault with-entrance discipline says the vault has exactly one access point, and that access point is hidden, locked, or both. Your own access to the vault is via a hidden door that looks like a wall, or via a teleport pad that you activate from inside your base, or via a trap that opens only when you trigger it with a specific pet.

Single-entrance discipline also applies to your own behavior. If you frequently enter and exit the vault, the entrance shows wear, and a careful raider can spot the wear and identify the vault. Use a different entrance for daily operations and reserve the real vault entrance for emergencies. The more often you use a hidden entrance, the less hidden it becomes.

### Vault Layers

A well-designed vault has at least three internal layers: an outer wall, an inner wall, and a pet enclosure. The outer wall is the strongest material, usually reinforced alloy. The inner wall is one tier lower but still durable. The pet enclosure is a small room inside the inner wall where the pets actually stand. The layers create distance between the raider and the pets, and each layer costs the raider time and resources to breach.

The space between layers is where you place traps. A the raider who is trying to navigate. A freeze trap in the pet enclosure stops the raider at the very last step, giving you time to respond or to let the trap deal maximum damage.

## Pet Placement Inside the Vault

How you place pets inside the vault affects both their security and your income. Pets in the deepest part of the vault are most secure but also hardest for you to access. Pets in the easiest-to-access part of the vault are less secure but more convenient. The standard approach is to place your most valuable pets in the deepest, hardest-to-reach section, and your moderately valuable pets in the easier sections.

### The Legendary Section

Legendary pets go in the deepest part of the vault. They are the most attractive targets for raiders and the most expensive to replace. Place them in a small enclosure surrounded by the strongest walls and the heaviest traps. Limit the number of legendary pets in any one enclosure, because losing one enclosure should not mean losing all your legendary pets. A common configuration is one legendary per small enclosure, with multiple small enclosures forming a wing of the vault.

### The Mythical Section

Mythical pets deserve an even more secure section than legendary pets. If you have multiple mythical pets, consider giving each one its own small enclosure with its own trap network. The cost pets between multiple enclosures, so a raider who studies the base does not know which enclosure currently contains the mythical.

### The High-Rare Section

High-rarity rare pets, those that are not legendary but still valuable, go in the middle layer of the vault. They are valuable enough to need protection but not so valuable that they belong with the legendary. Place them in a section with strong walls and standard traps, and accept that some of them will be lost in a major raid. The point of the high-rare section is to absorb losses that would otherwise hit the legendary section.

## Hidden Access Mechanisms

The vault entrance must be accessible to you and inaccessible to raiders. There are several mechanisms for achieving this, and the best vaults use more than one.

### The Hidden Door

A hidden door is a wall section that looks identical to the surrounding wall but opens when you walk near it with a specific pet or when you input a code. Hidden doors are common in mid-game bases because they are easy to build and reasonably effective. The downside is that hidden doors show wear. After a few months of use, the door's location becomes obvious to anyone watching your base for long periods. Rotate hidden door positions regularly to maintain the illusion.

### The Teleport Pad

A teleport pad moves you instantly from one location to another when detected by raiders with detection gear, and a detected teleport pad leads directly to the vault. Pair teleport pads with decoy teleport pads that lead to decoy rooms, so a raider who finds a teleport pad cannot tell which one leads to the real vault.

### The Trigger Pet

A trigger pet is a specific pet in your collection that, when held, grants access to the vault. The vault entrance is invisible unless the trigger pet is nearby. This is one of the most secure mechanisms, because a raider who steals your trigger pet still cannot access the vault unless they also know the vault's location. The downside is that you must keep the trigger pet safe, and if you lose it, you lose access to your own vault.

## Vault Maintenance

A vault requires ongoing maintenance. Walls decay, traps deplete, and pets may be moved as the meta changes. Schedule a weekly inspection of the vault, walking the layers and checking every wall, every trap, and every pet placement. Replace anything that has degraded. Move any pet that has changed value category. The vault you built six months ago is not the vault you need today.

### Wall Inspection

Walk the vault perimeter and check every wall section for visible damage. Even walls that have not been breached can next major raid. Replace it before that raid happens, not after.

### Trap Replenishment

Traps that have triggered recently show depletion in the management interface. Replenish them with new charges, or replace them entirely if they are too damaged to be reliable. A trap network that has triggered multiple times is a network that has saved your pets, and it deserves to be replenished. Skipping trap maintenance is a slow-motion failure that culminates in a single catastrophic raid.

### Pet Rotation

Rotate pets between sections based on current value. A pet that was legendary when you placed it in the deepest section may have been rebalanced to common. A pet that was common when you placed it in the outer layer may now be meta-relevant and worth promoting. Review the pet meta monthly and adjust placements accordingly. The vault should always reflect the current value of your pets, not the value they had when you first placed them.

## Emergency Vault Protocols

Even the best vault will eventually be breached. When that happens, having a pre-planned emergency protocol can mean the difference between losing one pet and losing everything.

### The Panic Move

The panic move is a pre-set teleport or a friend's base, where the raider cannot follow. The panic move requires advance setup and a trusted friend, but it is the ultimate backup when the primary vault is about to fall.

### The Decoy Sacrifice

If the breach is already inside the vault and you cannot relocate your pets in time, the decoy sacrifice buys time by offering the raider the decoy room's contents. Activate the decoy room's pet showcase, making it look like the valuable pets are still there. The raider grabs the decoy pets and leaves, believing they have hit the vault. The real pets remain in the hidden section. Decoy sacrifices are most effective against time-pressured raiders who do not search the entire vault.

### The Insurance Protocol

Maintain a small insurance stash of cash outside the vault at all times. The insurance stash is the cash you need to rebuild walls and traps after a major raid. Without it, a single catastrophic breach can leave you unable to defend your next session, which leads to a second breach, and so on. The insurance protocol is a simple rule: keep 10% of your liquid cash outside, the walls, the traps, the decoy rooms, all exist to protect the vault. The vault is the point. If you are not designing your base around the vault, you are designing it around the assumption that no one will ever seriously raid you, which is an assumption that fails in the first week of competitive play. Start with a simple vault in the center of your base, build outward from there, and refine the design as your collection grows. The vault that saves your collection is the vault you spent the most time planning, not the vault you built fastest.
