## Anti-Stealth Defense Patterns

Stealth raiders are defeated by detection and response speed, not by walls. A stealth raider who is detected in the first ten seconds of a raid is a stealth raider who will likely fail, because the defender can respond by relocating the valuables, locking the vault, or simply waiting out the raid timer. The anti-stealth defense patterns below maximize your detection capabilities.

### The Motion Sensor Net

A motion sensor net is a series of alarm traps placed in overlapping detection radii, so any moving raider triggers at least one of them. The net should cover every possible approach to the vault, including the obvious paths and the hidden ones. A motion sensor net is expensive to install, but it is the most reliable anti-stealth defense because no stealth gear in the game can suppress every sensor type. A. Layer multiple sensor types for full coverage.

The motion sensor net also has a psychological effect. A stealth raider who knows that sensors are watching will move more slowly, and slow movement is the enemy of stealth. The net does not need to catch every stealth raider; it just needs to make stealth raiders slow enough that the defender has time to respond.

### The Pressure Plate Carpet

A pressure plate carpet is a dense layer of alarm pressure plates covering the entire floor of a critical section. Pressure plates trigger when stepped on, regardless of whether the raider is moving or standing still. A pressure plate carpet is particularly effective against stealth raiders who wait in place to time their next move, because the wait itself triggers the alarm.

The downside of a pressure plate carpet is that it also triggers for the defender. The carpet must be designed so that the defender can disable individual plates or step over them, while raiders cannot. This is typically achieved by linking plates to a pet-based trigger, so only the defender's specific pet can cross the carpet silently.

The audio detection layer is particularly effective against stealth raiders who use silent cutters, because the silent cutter is silent in terms of movement noise but loud in terms of the wall it is cutting.

Place audio sensors near walls, especially the outer walls and the walls adjacent to the vault. A stealth raider who cuts a wall will trigger the audio sensor, alerting the defender to a breach attempt at the specific wall section.

## Anti-Brute-Force Defense Patterns

Brute-force raiders are defeated by cost and time. A brute-force raid that takes ten minutes and costs the raider more than the vault contains is a raid that will not be repeated. The anti-brute-force patterns below maximize the cost and time required for a successful breach.

### The Layered Wall Defense

The layered wall defense uses two parallel walls with a trap-filled gap between them. The outer wall absorbs the first wave of breaching damage. The trap layer in the gap damages the raider who has just breached the outer wall. The inner wall absorbs the second wave. A brute-force raider must breach all three components to reach the vault, which triples the time and resource cost of the raid.

The layered wall defense is most effective when the trap layer includes a slow trap, which holds the raider in the gap long enough to take damage from multiple sources. A slow trap followed by a spike trap followed resource attrition pattern is designed to drain the raider's gear charges rather than their health. Each layer of the base consumes one or more charges from the raider's gear, and by the time the raider reaches the vault, they have used most of their charges and cannot breach the final defenses. The pattern works against brute-force raiders because they rely on gear to power through walls, and once the gear is depleted, they are forced to retreat.

The most common resource attrition pattern places electric traps between wall layers. Electric traps damage gear and reduce charge efficiency. A raider who takes three electric trap hits will find their breaching gear performing at half capacity, which means each subsequent wall takes twice as long to breach. By the time they reach the vault, the raid timer has expired.

### The Decoy Vault Pattern

The decoy vault pattern is the ultimate anti-brute-force defense. A brute-force raider expects to reach the vault and grab the loot. If the vault they reach is a decoy, the raider has spent their entire raid budget on a fake. The real vault is somewhere else, often in a different section of the base or even outside the base entirely. The decoy vault pattern works because brute-force raiders are time-pressured and rarely search the entire base after reaching the apparent vault.

The decoy vault must so the raider feels they have reached a real prize. The decoy vault is the most expensive single defensive element in any base, but it is also the most effective against the most common raid type.

## Anti-Team Defense Patterns

Team raids are defeated by friction. Coordinated teams work well in open spaces and against simple defenses. They struggle in narrow corridors with overlapping trap coverage, because the narrow space prevents the team from spreading out and the traps damage the team collectively. The anti-team patterns below exploit this friction.

### The Maze Layout

A maze layout is a base design with multiple narrow corridors, dead ends, and switchbacks. The maze forces the team to either split up or move in a tight formation. If they split up, they lose coordination and become vulnerable to trap chains. If they stay together, they move slowly and trigger every trap in the path. The maze is the most effective single defense against team raids.

Maze layouts are expensive and time-consuming to build. They also make your own base harder to navigate, which is a real cost. Reserve the maze for the most valuable sections of the base, particularly around the vault. The outer perimeter can be a simple closed loop; the inner section can be a maze.

### Trap Cluster Synergy

A raider who enters a trap cluster takes damage from all directions simultaneously, which is more devastating than the same traps spread out. Trap clusters are particularly effective at choke points, where the team cannot avoid the cluster by spreading out.

The downside of trap clusters is cost. Five traps in one tile is expensive, and rebuilding after each raid is significant. Reserve trap clusters for the most critical chokepoints, particularly the entrance to the vault. One well-placed cluster can defeat a team raid on its own.

### The Disruption Pattern

The disruption pattern is designed to interfere with team coordination. The most common disruption element is a noise trap, which produces a loud sound that interferes with voice chat. A second disruption element is a vision trap, which obscures the screen and prevents the team from seeing each other's positions. Disruption patterns are most effective in the entrance corridor, where the team is most reliant on coordination to enter the base quickly.

## Adaptive Defense: Responding to the Raid Type

The best bases do not commit to a single defense pattern. They adapt to the raid type. A base that has anti-stealth sensors near the entrance, anti-brute-force layered walls in the middle, and anti-team maze corridors around the vault is prepared for any raid archetype. The cost is higher, but the resilience is significantly better.

### Reading the Raid in Real Time

When a raid begins is a stealth raid. If the raid is loud and the outer wall is taking heavy damage, it is a brute-force raid. If the raid has multiple breach points, it is a team raid. Each type calls for a different response.

For a stealth raid, the response is to lock the vault and wait out the timer. The stealth raider cannot breach a locked vault, and the timer will eventually force them to retreat. For a brute-force raid, the response is to activate the layered defense and the resource attrition pattern. The brute-force raider will eventually run out of resources. For a team raid, the response is to engage the maze and the trap cluster. The team will lose coordination in the maze.

### Defending Against Repeated Raids

If you are being raided repeatedly by the same player or the same team, the response is to redesign the base, not just to reinforce it. A repeated raider has studied your base and knows its weaknesses. Reinforcing the same design does not address the weaknesses, because the raider already knows them. Redesign the layout, change the vault location, rotate the pets, and add new defensive elements. The raider who has studied your base is now studying a different base.

## Conclusion: Defeat the Raid Type, Not the Raider

Counter-raider tactics in Build a Base and Steal are not about defeating individual players. They are about defeating the patterns those players use. Stealth patterns are defeated by detection. Brute-force patterns design when the patterns change. The base that survives the most raids is rarely the base with the strongest walls; it is the base that has the most appropriate defenses for the threats it actually faces.
