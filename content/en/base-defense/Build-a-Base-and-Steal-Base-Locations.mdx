The location of your base in Build a Base and Steal is one of the most underappreciated strategic decisions in the game. Most players choose their plot based on the spawn point, which means they end up in a high-traffic area with constant raider attention. Players who choose their plot deliberately end up in lower-traffic areas with more control over when and how they are raided. This guide covers the plot types available, the factors that affect each plot's defensibility, and the strategy for choosing the plot that fits your playstyle.

## The Plot Types

Build a Base and Steal offers several plot types in the game world, each with different characteristics that affect visibility, defensibility, and resource access. The plot types are categorized by their proximity to the central spawn area, their terrain, and their size.

The central plots are the plots closest to the spawn point. Central plots have the highest visibility, which means they are the most likely to be raided, but they also have the easiest access to the rolling machine, the gear shop, and the trading hub. Central plots are best for players who want to be active in the community and do not mind frequent raids.

The edge plots are the plots at the perimeter of the map. Edge plots have lower visibility, which means they are raided less frequently, but they are also farther from the central amenities. Edge plots require more walking to access the rolling machine and the shop, which adds a few minutes to every play session. Edge plots are best for players who want a quieter play experience and are willing to accept the inconvenience.

The corner plots are the plots at the very corner of the map, where two edges meet. Corner plots have the lowest visibility of any plot type, and they are the least likely to be raided. The downside is that corner plots are the farthest from the central amenities, and the walk to the rolling machine can take several minutes. Corner plots are best for players who have already established their collection and want to focus on defense and offline accumulation.

The hidden plots are the plots that are not visible from the main paths. Hidden plots are tucked behind terrain features or in areas that are not part of the main map. Hidden plots are the rarest and the most coveted, because they are the least likely to be raided. The downside is that hidden plots are also the hardest to find, and the developer occasionally removes them in updates to maintain map balance.

### The Visibility Factor

Visibility is the single most important factor in plot selection, because visibility directly affects raid frequency. A plot that is visible from the main paths will be seen by every player who walks past, and many of those players will consider raiding it. A plot that is hidden from the main paths will be seen by almost no one, and the raids will be few and far between.

The visibility factor is not constant. It changes based on the time of day (in game time), the current event, and the current player population. During events, the visibility of all plots increases, because more players are active. During quiet periods, the visibility of all plots decreases, because fewer players are online. The plot that is hidden during quiet periods may be visible during events, and the plot that is visible during quiet periods is definitely visible during events.

The visibility factor is also affected by your base design. A base with a tall wall, a bright color, or a distinctive feature is more visible than a base that blends into the terrain. Players who want to minimize visibility should choose a plot with natural camouflage and build a base that does not stand out. Players who want to maximize visibility (for trading or for social reasons) should choose a central plot and build a base that stands out.

### The Terrain Factor

The terrain of the plot affects the defensibility of the base. A flat plot is easy to build on but offers no natural defensive features. A plot with elevation is harder to build on but offers a natural defensive advantage, because raiders must approach from below. A plot with water or other natural barriers is even more defensible, because the barriers limit the directions from which raiders can approach.

The terrain factor is most important for new players, who may not have the resources to build a fully defensive base. A new player on a flat plot needs to spend cash on walls and traps to achieve basic defensibility. A new player on an elevated plot gets some defensibility for free, because the terrain provides the first layer of defense. The new player on an elevated plot can use their limited cash for higher-tier walls and traps, which makes the base more defensible overall.

The terrain factor is less important for experienced players, who have the resources to build a fully defensive base regardless of the terrain. The experienced player chooses a plot based on the other factors, knowing that they can compensate for any terrain disadvantage with construction.

### The Resource Access Factor

The proximity to the central amenities (rolling machine, gear shop, trading hub) affects how efficient your play sessions are. A player with a central plot can roll, shop, and trade without leaving the plot, which saves several minutes per session. A player with a corner plot must walk several minutes to reach the amenities, which adds time to every session.

The resource access factor is most important for active players, who interact with the amenities frequently. A player who plays 4 hours a day will spend significantly more time walking between the plot and the amenities than a player who plays 30 minutes a day. The active player should choose a central or near-central plot to minimize the walking time, while the casual player can choose a more distant plot without significant time cost.

The resource access factor is also affected by the teleport feature, which is unlocked at a certain progression level. The teleport feature allows you to instantly move to any amenity, regardless of the plot location. The teleport feature makes the plot location less important for resource access, because the walking time is eliminated. Players who have unlocked the teleport feature can choose their plot based on the other factors, knowing that the resource access is no longer a constraint.

### The Social Factor

The plot location also affects your social experience. A central plot is in the middle of the community, and you will frequently encounter other players, see their bases, and engage in social activities like trading and chatting. A corner plot is isolated, and you will rarely see other players, which means less social interaction but also less distraction.

The social factor is most important for players who enjoy the community aspect of Build a Base and Steal. A player who trades frequently, joins Discord raids, or participates in community events should choose a central plot to be near the social activity. A player who prefers solo play should choose a more isolated plot to avoid the distractions.

The social factor is also affected by the in-game chat, which is available regardless of plot location. Players who want social interaction can use the chat to talk to other players, even if they are in a corner plot. The chat is less rich than face-to-face interaction, but it allows for community engagement regardless of plot location.

## Choosing the Right Plot

The right plot depends on your playstyle and your goals. For new players, the recommended plot is a near-central plot with moderate visibility. The new player wants some social interaction and some raiding activity, but does not want to be the target of every passing player. A near-central plot provides the balance.

For mid-game players, the recommended plot is a mid-distance plot with moderate terrain features. The mid-game player has built a base worth defending, and they want a plot that gives them defensibility without isolating them from the community. A mid-distance plot with elevation or natural barriers is ideal.

For end-game players, the recommended plot is a hidden plot with strong terrain features. The end-game player has a large, valuable collection and wants to minimize the raid frequency. A hidden plot with natural barriers is the most defensible option, and the end-game player can afford the inconvenience of being far from the amenities.

## Plot Migration

The plot you choose at the start of the game is not permanent. Most servers allow you to migrate your base to a new plot for a cash cost, which is usually based on the size of your base. The migration cost is significant, but it is justified when the new plot provides meaningful strategic advantages.

Plot migration is most common when players reach a new progression milestone. A new player who started in a central plot may migrate to an edge plot after building a valuable collection, because the collection needs better protection. A mid-game player who started in an edge plot may migrate to a hidden plot after acquiring legendary pets, because the legendaries need the best protection available.

The migration should be planned carefully, because it is a significant undertaking. The base must be disassembled in the old plot and reassembled in the new plot, which takes time and may expose the base during the transition. The migration should be done during a quiet period, when other players are unlikely to raid the disassembled base, and it should be done with a reserve of cash to cover the migration cost.
