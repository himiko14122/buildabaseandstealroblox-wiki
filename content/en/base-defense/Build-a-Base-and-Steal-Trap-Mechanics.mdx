Traps are the active defense of Build a Base and Steal, the mechanism that turns a static wall into a real threat to raiders. While walls slow raiders down, traps damage them, debuff them, and force them to make difficult decisions about how to spend their raid time. A base with well-placed traps is significantly harder to raid than a base with the same walls but no traps, and the difference is most pronounced when the trap placement is layered and strategic. This guide covers every trap type in the game, the mechanics of each, and the optimal placement for maximum effectiveness.

## Trap Categories

Traps in Build a Base and Steal fall into three broad categories: damage traps, debuff traps, and utility traps. Each category serves a different purpose, and the most effective trap layouts use a mix of all three.

Damage traps deal direct damage to the raider. The damage is usually inflicted over time or as a single burst when the trap is triggered. The damage depletes the raider's health, and if it depletes enough, the raider is forced to retreat or use healing items. Damage traps are the most common and the most straightforward.

Debuff traps do not deal damage directly, but they apply negative effects to the raider. The effects include slowing the raider, reducing their damage output, blinding them, or otherwise impairing their ability to complete the raid. Debuff traps are most effective when combined with damage traps, because the debuff keeps the raider in the damage zone longer.

Utility traps provide defensive benefits that are not directly related to damaging the raider. The benefits include revealing hidden raiders, slowing the extraction of stolen pets, teleporting the raider to a different part of the base, or creating barriers that block the raider's path. Utility traps are situational but can be very effective in the right context.

### Spike Trap

The spike trap is the most basic damage trap and the first trap most players unlock. The spike trap is triggered when a raider steps on the trigger zone, and it deals a burst of damage to the raider. The damage is moderate, the cooldown is short, and the cost is low.

The optimal placement for spike traps is at the entrance to your base, where the raider is most exposed and most likely to trigger the trap before they have a chance to react. A single spike trap at the entrance is the most basic defensive feature, and it is required for any serious base.

The weakness of the spike trap is its low damage. A raider with a full health bar and a healing item can absorb the spike trap and continue. The trap is most effective when it is the first of multiple traps, because the raider who takes damage from the spike trap is more cautious and more likely to trigger the next trap.

### Poison Trap

The poison trap is a damage-over-time trap that applies a poison effect to the raider. The poison effect deals damage over several seconds, and the effect stacks with other poison traps. The poison damage is lower per second than the spike trap, but the total damage over the duration is higher.

The optimal placement for poison traps is in chokepoints and other areas where the raider cannot avoid the effect. A raider who walks into a poison trap cannot escape the damage for the duration of the effect, and the damage accumulates. The poison trap is also effective against groups of raiders, because the poison affects everyone in the trigger zone.

The weakness of the poison trap is that the damage is delayed, which gives the raider time to use a healing item. The trap is most effective when the raider is already at low health from a previous trap, because the poison damage can finish them off before they can heal.

### Slow Trap

The slow trap is a debuff trap that reduces the raider's movement speed for several seconds after triggering. The slow effect does not deal damage, but it makes the raider take longer to navigate the base, which gives your other traps more time to trigger. The slow trap is one of the most cost-effective traps in the game, because its cost is low and its effect is significant.

The optimal placement for slow traps is in the middle of your base, where the raider has already committed to the raid and is now slowed down. The slowed raider is more vulnerable to damage traps that they would otherwise run past, and they take longer to reach the extraction point, which gives you more time to respond.

The weakness of the slow trap is that it does not deal damage directly, which means a careful raider can use it without losing much. The trap is most effective when combined with damage traps, because the slowed raider cannot avoid the damage as easily.

### Blind Trap

The blind trap is a debuff trap that reduces the raider's vision for several seconds after triggering. The blind effect makes the raider unable to see the layout of the base clearly, which makes it harder for them to navigate and easier for them to trigger other traps. The blind trap is one of the most psychologically effective traps, because the loss of vision is disorienting.

The optimal placement for blind traps is at decision points in the base, where the raider needs to choose a path. The blinded raider cannot see the options clearly and is more likely to make the wrong choice, which often leads them into additional traps. The blind trap is also effective near your valuable pets, because the blinded raider cannot see the extraction prompt and may miss the opportunity to extract.

The weakness of the blind trap is that it does not deal damage directly, and the effect is short. The trap is most effective in layered defenses, where the blind is just one of several effects the raider has to deal with.

### Alarm Trap

The alarm trap is a utility trap that alerts you when triggered. The alarm appears as a notification, and it tells you the location of the triggering raider. The alarm does not deal damage or apply a debuff, but it gives you information that you can use to respond to the raid.

The optimal placement for alarm traps is at the entrance to your base, so you are alerted the moment a raid begins. The alarm gives you time to log in, observe the raid, and potentially intervene. The alarm is also useful in the middle of the base, because the second alarm tells you the raider has breached the outer layer and is now in the middle buffer.

The weakness of the alarm trap is that it requires you to be online and able to respond. If you are offline or unable to log in quickly, the alarm does not help. The trap is most effective for players who log in frequently, because they can use the alarm to coordinate their defense.

## Extraction Delay Trap

The extraction delay trap is a utility trap that increases the time it takes to extract a pet. The delay effect is applied when the raider begins the extraction, and it adds several seconds to the extraction time. The delay does not prevent the extraction, but it gives you more time to respond or for the raid timer to expire.

The optimal placement for extraction delay traps is near your most valuable pets, where the delay is most impactful. The delayed extraction gives you time to log in, and it makes the raid timer a more significant constraint. The trap is also effective psychologically, because the raider who realizes the extraction is taking longer is more likely to abandon the raid.

The weakness of the extraction delay trap is that it only affects the extraction step, not the breach. The trap is most effective in bases with strong walls, because the raider takes a long time to reach the extraction point in the first place.

## Trap Combinations

The most effective trap layouts combine multiple trap types in a sequence that forces the raider to deal with all of them. A typical sequence is a slow trap followed by a damage trap followed by a blind trap. The raider walks into the slow trap and is slowed down. The slowed raider then walks into the damage trap and takes damage. The damaged raider then walks into the blind trap and is blinded, which makes the next trap in the sequence even more effective.

The combinations should be placed in chokepoints, where the raider cannot avoid them. The chokepoint is the most effective location for trap combinations, because the raider is forced to walk through the entire sequence. A raider who triggers the first trap in a chokepoint will trigger all of them, and the cumulative effect is much stronger than any individual trap.

The combinations should also be layered, with one combination in the outer perimeter, one in the middle buffer, and one in the inner vault. The layered combinations force the raider to deal with multiple trap sequences, and the cumulative cost is usually enough to deter even determined raiders.

## Trap Maintenance

Traps require maintenance after they are triggered, and the maintenance has a cost. The maintenance includes rearming the trap (which costs cash) and replacing the trap if it is destroyed. The maintenance cost is part of the defensive budget, and it should be factored into the 30% defensive allocation.

The maintenance schedule depends on the trap type. Spike traps require frequent rearming because they trigger often. Slow traps require less frequent rearming because they trigger less often. The most cost-effective maintenance schedule is to rearm all traps after every successful defense, so the base is fully prepared for the next raid.
