The offline money system is the single most important economic mechanic in Build a Base and Steal. It is what separates a player who logs in once a day from a player who has to grind for six hours straight to keep up. Every pet you own, every wall you build, and every upgrade you purchase feeds into the same underlying income formula, and that formula keeps running whether you are at your keyboard, in school, or asleep. Understanding exactly how offline money works is the first step toward turning the game into a true passive empire.

## The Core Income Formula

Every pet in Build a Base and Steal generates a baseline cash amount per second, and that amount is multiplied by the rarity tier, the level of the pet, and any active collection bonuses. The base rate for a common pet sits at 1 cash per second. An uncommon pet generates 1.5, a rare generates 3, a legendary generates 8, and a mythic generates 20. These numbers look small in isolation, but they compound across a full pet collection, and that compounding is the foundation of every player's economy.

The formula also includes a "presence multiplier" that kicks in when you are online. While you are actively playing, your effective income is doubled compared to offline. This is by design: the developers reward active play so that the game remains engaging, but they do not punish players who have to step away. The transition between online and offline rates happens instantly the moment you close the client or the moment you log back in.

### Level and Upgrade Multipliers

Each pet can be leveled up using pet-specific upgrade tokens, and every ten levels increases the income rate by 25 percent. A level 50 common pet therefore generates the same income as an unleveled uncommon pet, which means upgrading the pets you already own is often a better investment than chasing rarer pets. The same logic applies to collection bonuses, which stack multiplicatively rather than additively.

## How the Offline Cap Works

Build a Base and Steal imposes a hard cap on how much offline income you can accumulate in a single session. The default cap is 8 hours, meaning that even if you log off for 36 hours, you will only collect cash for the first 8 hours of that absence. This cap prevents the most dedicated idle players from runaway economies, and it keeps the active player base competitive with players who only check in once a day.

The cap is calculated from the moment your client disconnects, not from the moment you stop moving in-game. If you close the game, the timer starts. If you stay in the game but stop interacting, the timer does not start, but your pets are generating at the lower offline rate. The practical implication is that you should never leave the game running in the background hoping to grind passively; the higher online rate only applies when the client sees you as active.

### Bypassing the Cap with Boosts

There are two legitimate ways to extend the offline cap. The first is the Premium Pass, which raises the cap to 16 hours. The second is the time-limited "Extended Rest" boost, which is occasionally granted through codes, events, or the daily login reward track. Both are worth using strategically: if you know you will be away for a long weekend, activate Extended Rest before you log off so that the boost covers the entire absence rather than the first eight hours.

## Maximizing Your Offline Earnings

The fastest way to increase your offline income is to fill your pet storage. The income formula is roughly linear in the number of pets, which means a player with 50 common pets will out-earn a player with 5 legendary pets during most of the early game. Storage is a real bottleneck here. The default storage cap is 30 pets, and expanding it to 60 or 100 costs progressively more cash, but the storage expansion almost always pays for itself within a week of active play.

Second, prioritize leveling your highest-rarity pets. The level multiplier is more impactful on legendaries and mythics than on commons, because the base rate is already higher. Spending upgrade tokens on a common is rarely worth it, while spending the same tokens on a legendary can double or triple its effective output. Most experienced players hold their upgrade tokens in reserve until they roll a legendary, then dump everything into it.

### Timing Your Logouts

Logging out immediately after a major upgrade session is one of the oldest tricks in the Build a Base and Steal playbook. Because the offline timer starts when your client disconnects, the smart play is to finish all of your upgrades, claim your daily rewards, and then log off so that the offline clock runs against your maximum rate. Logging off in the middle of an upgrade session, by contrast, wastes the offline window because you are still ramping up.

## Common Misconceptions

The biggest misconception about offline money is that it is separate from your active income. They use the exact same formula, and your offline rate is just your active rate with a 0.5 multiplier applied. If you want to know your offline rate, watch your cash counter for one minute while you are online and then divide by 120. The result is your offline rate per second.

Another common misconception is that pets inside walls generate more income than pets outside walls. They do not. Walls only protect against raids. The income rate is determined entirely by pet rarity, level, and collection bonuses, regardless of where the pet is placed in your base. Move your pets freely without worrying about income loss.

### The Myth of the Overnight Trick

Some players claim that logging off and back on resets the offline cap. It does not. The cap is calculated server-side, and reconnecting simply triggers the payout of whatever is currently accumulated. You cannot game the system by disconnecting and reconnecting repeatedly, and attempting to do so is just wasted effort. Trust the formula, log off when you are done, and let the cash flow in.
