The difference between an average offline income and a top-tier offline income in Build a Base and Steal comes down to one thing: multiplier stacking. Most players collect from their pets and walk away with whatever the system gives them. Advanced players learn the precise order in which auras, happiness objects, event bonuses, and pet placement bonuses apply, and they arrange their base so that every multiplier compounds on top of the others. This guide walks through each multiplier in Build a Base and Steal and shows you the a Base and Steal applies bonuses in a strict order, and understanding that order is the key to optimization. The first multiplier applied is the per-pet base rate, which is fixed by the pet's rarity and cannot be modified directly. The second is the pet's individual happiness score. The third is the aura bonus from objects within the base. The fourth is the event bonus, if one is active. The fifth is the server-wide happiness modifier, which fluctuates based on how recently a raid occurred in the server. Each multiplier multiplies the result of the previous one, so small gains at the bottom of the stack compound into large gains at the top.

The exact formula reads: `Final Income = Base Rate × (Happiness ÷ 100) × (1 + Aura Bonus) × (1 + Event Bonus) × Server Mood`. The server mood term is the only one you cannot directly control, but the other four are all under your influence. The order is important: happiness is calculated per-pet, aura is calculated per-base, and event bonus is calculated server-wide. This means a 20% happiness improvement and a 20% aura improvement do not add to a 40% bonus — they multiply to a 44% total bonus. Multiplicative stacking is your friend, and the game is designed to reward players who learn to exploit it.

### Per-Pet Happiness as the Foundation

Happiness is the most important multiplier to optimize because it has the largest range around 100-130%, because they place pets and forget about them. The advanced play is to actively maintain happiness through a daily feeding routine and to use happiness objects that boost specific pet types.

Happiness objects in Build a Base and Steal come in three categories: feeders, beds, and play areas. Feeders boost food-type pets. Beds boost comfort-type pets. Play areas boost energy-type pets. Each happiness object gives a flat 30-50% boost to all eligible pets within its radius, with diminishing returns for overlapping objects of the same type. The optimal configuration uses one of each type per zone, with no overlaps, to maximize coverage without wasting the diminishing-returns budget.

## Aura Placement Strategy

Auras are the second-largest multiplier and the easiest to overlook. Each aura object in Build a Base and Steal contributes a small passive bonus to all pets within its radius, and the bonuses stack additively across multiple auras. A base with three auras can easily double the effective income of a base with zero auras, even if both bases have identical pets and identical happiness scores. The most common aura sources are lamps, fountains, banners, music boxes, and seasonal decorations.

A the minimum number of objects. A well-designed Build a Base and Steal base has three to five aura objects, each placed so that its circle covers the densest possible cluster of high-value pets. The worst mistake is to spread auras evenly across the base; this leaves gaps that some pets will never benefit from, while the aura contribution to any single pet remains low.

### The Three-Aura Sweet Spot

Empirical testing shows that the third aura provides more marginal value than the fourth, fifth, or sixth, because three auras can be arranged to cover nearly every pet in a typical Build a Base and Steal base without excessive overlap. Beyond three, additional auras produce diminishing returns as the overlap with existing auras grows. The optimal setup is three auras placed in a triangle pattern around your highest-value pet cluster, with the pet cluster itself positioned at the geometric center of the triangle.

The three-aura sweet spot also keeps your base's footprint manageable. Build a Base and Steal bases are constrained by the available plot size, and auras cost plot space. Spending 20% of your plot on auras is acceptable; spending 40% is not,aces two pets is a net loss if the displaced pets are worth more than the aura bonus covers.

## Event Bonus Timing

Event bonuses in Build a Base and Steal are the largest single multiplier in the game, often applying a 2-5x bonus to all offline income during the event window. The catch is that events are short — usually three to seven days — and the bonus applies only while the event is active. The advanced play is to time your roster upgrades and base expansions to coincide with events, so that the bonus multiplies a larger base.

Specifically, the optimal event strategy is to log in at the start of an event, immediately spend any cash you have on pet rolls or aura upgrades, and then log out. The events multiply the value of every pet you add during the event, so a 2x event bonus on a legendary roll effectively halves the cost of that legendary in real terms. A player who plans their upgrades around events can effectively cut their progression cost in half over a long timeline.

### Stacking Multiple Events

Build a Base and Steal occasionally runs two events simultaneously, with one event multiplier applied to income and another applied to rolling rates. When this happens, the optimal play, while the income bonus makes the new pets worth more in passive income. The window is short, usually 48-72 hours, so preparation matters: have your cash stockpile ready before the stacked event starts.

Outside of stacked events, the optimal play is to focus on one or the other. If the active event is an income event, do not roll during it — spend your time reinforcing defenses and arranging auras. If the active event is a rolling event, spend everything on rolls and accept that your offline income will be lower for a few days until the new pets mature into the base. The skill is in reading the event type and adjusting the routine accordingly.

## Server Mood and Raid Timing

Server mood is the one multiplier you cannot directly control, but you can influence it indirectly through raid timing. After a successful raid in your server, the server mood briefly dips, reducing all offline income by 10-20% for the next several hours. The dip is steeper for the victim than for the attacker, but it applies globally. The advanced play is to log out immediately after a successful raid, because the dip window is the worst time to accumulate offline income, and returning to a fresh the past few hours — produces the best offline income, because the server mood is at its baseline of 1.0. Build a Base and Steal servers are not random: the more time has passed since the last raid, the higher the server mood climbs. Logging off for 12 hours during a stable window can produce noticeably more income than logging off for 12 hours immediately after a raid.

### The Logging Window

The ideal logging window in Build a Base and Steal is 8-12 hours during a period of high server mood. This window balances two factors: long enough to accumulate substantial income, short enough to reset the 24-hour cap before hitting diminishing returns on the cap. Players who log in once every 24 hours actually lose income compared to players who log in twice every 24 hours, because the second login resets the cap and starts a fresh accumulation window.

For most working adults, the natural logging window is overnight. Log out before bed, log in when you wake up. This produces a 6-10 hour window that fits perfectly within the cap curve. The second login in the middle of the day, even if it is only a five-minute collection and a quick log-out, doubles the effective income for the day. The math is simple: two short sessions beat one long session, toward high rarity, all pets at 150%+ happiness, three auras arranged in a triangle, active event bonus, premium cap extension, and a stable server mood during the logging window. A player who achieves this configuration consistently can expect 5-8x the offline income of a player who simply collects and walks away. The setup is not cheap, but the cash recovered from a single well-timed event often pays for the entire build.
