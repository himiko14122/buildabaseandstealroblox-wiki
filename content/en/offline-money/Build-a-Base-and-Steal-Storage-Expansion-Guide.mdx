Pet storage is the bottleneck that holds back most new players in Build a Base and Steal. You start the game with 30 storage slots, which feels like plenty until you actually start rolling seriously. By the time you have played for a week, you will be cycling pets in and out of storage constantly, and the players who expand their storage earliest are the ones who pull ahead in offline money. This guide covers the exact cost curve, the income math, and the optimal timing for every storage upgrade.

## The Default Storage and Why It Is Not Enough

The default pet storage in Build a Base and Steal is 30 slots. That sounds generous, but it includes the pets you have placed in your base, the pets in your "ready to deploy" inventory, and the pets you have flagged for trade. Once you account for the realistic split between base, inventory, and trading, you are working with maybe 20 to 22 effective slots in the early game. That is enough to host one or two families plus a buffer of extras, but it is not enough to host a competitive mid-game roster.

The first sign that you need to expand is when you start seeing the "Storage Full" message after a roll. The second sign is when you start making active decisions about which pets to delete, rather than keeping everything. Both signs are common within the first three to five days of play for a new account, and ignoring them costs you real income.

## The Cost Curve

Pet storage expansions in Build a Base and Steal follow a roughly exponential cost curve. The first upgrade, from 30 to 40 slots, costs a few thousand cash and is essentially free for any active player. The second upgrade, from 40 to 60, costs roughly 25,000 cash. The third, from 60 to 100, costs 100,000 cash. The fourth, from 100 to 200, costs 500,000 cash. After that, expansions continue to scale but the developer introduces "Premium" tiers that cost Robux or limited event currency.

The exact numbers shift with patches, but the shape of the curve is constant. Each tier is roughly 4x to 5x the previous one, which means the early tiers are extremely cost-effective and the late tiers are luxury purchases. The sweet spot for most players is the 100-slot tier, which is the highest cash-only upgrade and provides enough headroom to host a competitive late-game roster.

### Storage Cost vs Income Payoff

The income payoff from a storage expansion is roughly linear in the number of new slots. Adding 10 slots gives you 10 more pets, which gives you 10 more income generators, which produces a stable long-term income boost. The exact amount of boost depends on what you put in those slots, but a reasonable estimate is 5 to 15 percent of your current offline rate per 10 slots added, when filled with appropriate pets.

The math is straightforward: if your current offline rate is 100 cash per second and you add 10 slots, then fill them with A-Tier pets, your new offline rate will be around 130 to 150 cash per second. Over a week, that difference adds up to several hundred thousand cash, which is far more than the cost of the upgrade. Storage expansions almost always pay for themselves within a week of filling the new slots.

## When to Expand

The optimal time to expand your pet storage is the moment you can comfortably afford the next tier without going broke. This sounds obvious, but many new players hoard cash waiting for "the right moment" that never comes. The right moment is when you have 1.5x to 2x the cost of the upgrade in your wallet and no immediate need for that cash elsewhere. If you meet those conditions, expand.

The worst time to expand is when you are saving for a specific pet or a base upgrade. Storage is a long-term investment, but specific pets and base upgrades are short-term goals. If you have a legendary pet chasing window opening in an hour, spend the cash on rolls instead of storage. The pet will generate income for as long as you keep it, but only if you actually roll it.

### Priority Order for Cash Spending

A reasonable priority order for new players in the mid-game is: storage expansion first, then a single defensive wall upgrade, then more storage, then a single offensive gear purchase, then more storage. The pattern repeats until you hit the cash-only cap, at which point you switch to defensive and offensive priorities for the rest of the game. Storage is the foundation of offline money, and the rest of the game builds on top of that foundation.

One exception to this rule is during active events. If a limited-time event offers a 2x or 3x income multiplier, you want as many pets in storage as possible during the event window. The right move is to expand storage right before the event starts, not after. Players who plan ahead for events routinely double or triple their typical offline income from a single weekend.

## Filling New Storage Efficiently

Once you have expanded your storage, the next question is what to put in it. The naive answer is "more pets," but the smarter answer is "the right pets." A newly expanded storage is a great opportunity to clean out duplicates, complete families, and slot in B-Tier pets you have been ignoring. A player who carefully curates their new slots will see a larger income boost than a player who fills them with whatever happens to drop first.

The recommended approach is to set a goal for each new storage tier. When you expand from 30 to 40 slots, your goal might be to complete one full uncommon family. When you expand from 40 to 60, your goal might be to slot in the entire S-Tier and A-Tier rosters you have been cycling in and out. Each expansion should be paired with a roster improvement goal, not just a number increase.

### Trading to Fill Gaps

Trading is the fastest way to fill new storage efficiently. Instead of rolling hundreds of times hoping for the exact pet you need, you can offer duplicates to other players who have the pet you want. Most trades in Build a Base and Steal are 2 commons for 1 uncommon, or 1 rare plus 1 uncommon for 1 legendary. The exact rates fluctuate with the trading market, but the principle is consistent: your duplicates are worth more to a player trying to complete a family than they are to you, sitting in storage.

Active traders routinely shave weeks off their family completion times by leveraging the trading market. If you have a serious expansion plan, check the trading Discord at least once per day and look for trades that move you toward your family completion goals.
