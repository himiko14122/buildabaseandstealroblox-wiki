The income system in Build a Base and Steal looks simple on the surface: roll pets, place them in your base, watch the cash counter tick up. Under the hood, however, the math is layered enough that even experienced players regularly miscalculate their effective rates. This guide breaks down every multiplier in the system, shows the exact formulas, and gives you the practical shortcuts used by the top 1 percent of players to squeeze base rate, measured in cash per second. The base rates in Build a Base and Steal are: common 1.0, uncommon 1.5, rare 3.0, legendary 8.0, mythic 20.0. These are the "headline" numbers that the pet's tooltip displays, but they are the start of the calculation, not the end. The actual income a pet generates is its base rate multiplied by every active bonus, which typically includes: a level multiplier, a collection bonus, a server event multiplier, a Premium Pass multiplier, and a few rarer boosts.

The order of operations matters. Multipliers stack multiplicatively, which means a 1.5x and a 1.3x do not add up to 2.8x; they multiply to 1.95x. This is friendly to players who stack many small bonuses, because small bonuses compound meaningfully across a large roster. It is also a reminder that no single multiplier dominates the calculation, and the path to maximum income is to optimize all of them rather than focusing on one.

### The Level Multiplier

The level multiplier in Build a Base and Steal is calculated as 1 + (0.025 × level), capped at level 100. That means a level 1 pet has a multiplier of 1.025x, a level 50 pet has a 2-month project, but the income gain at level 100 is 3.5x the level 1 baseline, which is significant.

The most efficient way to level pets is to use the daily free level-up token, which every player receives. The free token grants 5 levels per use, and it stacks with paid tokens, so the order of operations is: collect your daily token, then spend your paid tokens on the same pet if you want to push it faster. Tokens spent on multiple pets dilutes the multiplier and is generally less efficient than stacking them on one.

## Collection Bonuses and Family Math

Collection bonuses are flat multipliers that apply to every pet in a completed family. A 1.2x family bonus, for example, means every pet in that family produces 1.2x its calculated income. The bonus is multiplicative with the level multiplier, so a level 50 pet inside a 1.2x family produces 2.25x × 1.2x = 2.7x the base rate.

The key insight is that the family bonus applies to every pet in the family, not just one. This means that completing a family of 4 pets with a 1.2x bonus effectively gives you a 1.2x multiplier on 4 different pets' income, which compounds across the family. ## Server Event Multipliers

Server events in Build a Base and Steal are limited-time windows where the entire server enjoys a boosted income rate. The most common events are 2x weekends and 3x launch celebrations, but there are also smaller events tied to player milestones and update launches. The multiplier applies to both online and offline income, but offline income during an event only counts if the event is still running when you reconnect.

Stacking a 2x event with the Premium Pass and a absences around the largest event windows, because that is when the offline cap is most valuable. An 8-hour offline window during a 3x event is equivalent to a 24-hour offline window at the normal rate, in pure income terms.

### Boost Stacking Order

Boosts do not all stack in the same way. Family bonuses, level multipliers, and base rates are all "passive" multipliers that apply automatically. Event multipliers, Premium Pass multipliers, and code-granted boosts are "active" multipliers that require specific conditions to be met. The order in which you activate active multipliers does not matter; they all stack multiplicatively. What does matter is the duration: an 8-hour boost that ends mid-window is the same as no boost at all for the second half of the window.

The optimal strategy for boost stacking is to activate all active boosts at the start of a play session, then log off only after the longest boost has expired. This ensures that you collect the full benefit of every boost and avoid wasting any of the active duration.

### The Lifetime Income Formula

If you want to calculate the lifetime income of a single pet, multiply its base rate by 0.5 (the offline multiplier) by 28,800 (seconds in the cap) by 365 (days in a year). For a level 1 common pet, that is 1 × 0.5 × 28,800 × 365 = 5,256,000 cash per year. For a level 50 legendary, that is 8 × 2.25 × 0.5 × 28,800 × 365 = 94,608,000 cash per year. The gap between the two is roughly 18x, which is why investing in high-rarity pets and high levels pays off over the long term, even if the daily cash difference feels small.
