Build a **Base** and Steal's offline income system looks simple on the surface — log off, log back in, collect a pile of cash — but underneath the surface sits a layered set of formulas, multipliers, and diminishing returns curves that can multiply or destroy your passive earnings depending on how well you understand them. This guide breaks down every public formula, every confirmed multiplier, and every soft cap so that you can build a roster specifically optimized to extract the maximum amount of cash from every second you are not playing.

## The Base Formula

Every pet in Build a Base and Steal contributes to your offline income through a simple base formula: `Base Income = Pet Rate × Pet Multiplier × Server Happiness × Aura Bonus`. The first three terms are visible in the pet inspection screen, but the Aura Bonus is a hidden fourth term that most players never notice. Auras are placed objects in your base — lamps, fountains, banners — and each one contributes a small additive bonus to all pets within its radius. A fully decked-out base with three overlapping auras can increase offline income by 25-40% without adding a single pet to the roster.

The Pet Rate is the foundation. Common pets generate 1-3 cash per second. Uncommon generate 3-10. Rare generate 10-30. Epic generate 30-80. Legendary generate 80-200. Mythic pets start at 200 and scale up to 1,000+ for the very top tier. These numbers are baseline values for a single pet with no modifiers and a neutral happiness score.

### Reading the Inspection Screen

When you tap a pet in Build a Base and Steal, the inspection screen shows three numbers: base rate, current happiness, and effective rate. Most players read only the effective rate and skip the math. That is a mistake, because the difference between base and effective reveals which bonuses are currently active. If your effective rate is significantly higher than the base, you have aura or happiness bonuses working for you. If they are identical, your setup is leaving money on the table and a small rearrangement could double your offline income.

The inspection screen also shows a "rate per minute" estimate that extrapolates your current production to a one-minute window. This number is misleading for offline calculation purposes, because offline income uses a different time scale than active play. Do not trust the rate-per-minute number for anything longer than a few real-time minutes.

## Happiness: The Hidden Multiplier

Happiness is the single most misunderstood mechanic in Build a Base and Steal. It is a per-pet score that ranges from 0% to 200% and multiplies the pet's effective income. A legendary pet at 200% happiness generates twice as much as the same legendary at 100% happiness. A mythic at 0% happiness generates nothing. Happiness is determined by pet placement: pets near a feeder, a bed, or a play area receive bonuses, while pets isolated in the corner of your base slowly drift toward 100%.

The happiness system is also subject to diminishing returns. Going from 50% to 100% happiness gives you a 2x multiplier. Going from 100% to 150% gives you a 1.5x. Going from 150% to 200% gives you only a 1.33x. The first 100% is the cheapest happiness to earn and the highest-leverage place to invest your time. Most competitive players cap their setups at 150% per pet because the final 50% requires so many happiness objects that it is not worth the loss of pet slots.

### How to Manipulate Happiness Quickly

The fastest way to max happiness is the "feeding burst" technique. When you feed a pet in Build a Base and Steal, its happiness jumps by 20-30 points for several minutes. During that window, you can collect offline income at the boosted rate. Some players log in, feed every pet, collect, and log out within a two-minute window. This strategy effectively turns a small food investment into a substantial short-term passive income boost. It is especially effective right before a long real-world absence.

The second technique is the "rotation" trick. Pets gradually lose happiness over time, but the rate of loss slows as happiness approaches 100%. If you log in every 12 hours and rotate pets through your highest-tier happiness objects, you can keep the entire roster near 100% without ever having to feed anyone. The rotation order matters: place your most valuable pets in the highest-bonus slots first, then cascade down.

## Diminishing Returns on Pet Count

Build a Base and Steal applies a soft logarithmic curve to pet stacking. The first pet in your base contributes its full rate. The second contributes about 95% of its rate. The third about 90%. By the time you have ten pets, each additional pet is contributing only 50-60% of its nominal rate, and by the time you have thirty pets, each new pet contributes less than 30%. This curve is why "all-in on mythics" beats "wide roster of commons" for serious players, even though the wide roster looks more impressive.

The formula, as reverse-engineered from observed behavior, looks roughly like: `Effective Pet Rate = Base Rate × (1 - 0.05 × ln(Pet Count + 1))`. This means every doubling of your roster costs you roughly 7% of effective income per pet. Doubling from 10 to 20 pets reduces per-pet effectiveness by about 7%. Doubling from 20 to 40 reduces it by another 7%. The compounding effect means that a focused 20-pet roster can out-earn a sprawling 60-pet roster using the same total pet value.

### When to Stop Adding Pets

The sweet spot for most Build a Base and Steal players is a roster of 15-25 pets, weighted heavily toward the highest rarity they can sustain. Going below 15 leaves the diminishing-returns curve underutilized, while going above 25 dilutes your average per-pet rate. The exact sweet spot depends on your server's pet prices and your ability to refresh legendaries, but the principle holds: fewer, better pets out-earn many mediocre pets in offline scenarios.

The exception is the event window. During special events, Build a Base and Steal sometimes flattens the diminishing-returns curve or even inverts it. Events are the one time when stacking 30+ pets becomes correct, because the event bonus is calculated per-pet rather than per-roster. Always check the active event page before deciding whether to trim or expand your roster.

## The Offline Cap Curve

Offline income in Build a Base and Steal is capped at 24 hours of effective accumulation. After 24 hours, your pets continue to produce but the income does not grow further until you log in and reset the cap. This cap interacts with the diminishing-returns curve in an interesting way: a focused roster of 20 high-rarity pets hits the cap in 8-12 hours, while a sprawling roster of 60 pets takes 20-24 hours to hit the same cap. The smaller roster is more efficient in the long run because you reset the cap more often and avoid the wasted accumulation time at the end of the cap window.

The cap resets the moment you log in and collect. The reset is global, meaning it applies to all pets simultaneously. This is why a "two short sessions per day" routine outperforms a "one long session" routine for most players. Logging in twice gives you two collection windows, each resetting the cap and starting a fresh 24-hour accumulation cycle. The result is that a player who logs in twice daily can collect up to 48 hours of effective income in a 24-hour real-time window, effectively doubling their passive rate.

### Beyond the Cap: Premium Buffs

Build a Base and Steal offers a premium currency option that extends the offline cap from 24 to 72 hours. The premium extension is mathematically worth it for any player who cannot log in for more than one session per day, because the extra 48 hours of accumulation dwarfs the cost of the premium pass after the first week. For players who can log in twice daily, the premium extension is less compelling, because the natural cap is already larger than their typical absence.

The premium extension also stacks with the natural reset behavior. If you have the premium extension active and log in once every 48 hours, you collect 48 hours of accumulation per session, which is exactly twice what a non-premium player gets. If you log in once every 72 hours, you collect the full 72-hour cap. Going beyond 72 hours still wastes accumulation, so even premium players benefit from a consistent login schedule.

## Stacking Multipliers: The Big Picture

The maximum theoretical offline income in Build a Base and Steal is achieved by stacking every multiplier simultaneously: full 200% happiness, three overlapping auras, a 20-pet focused roster, two login cycles per day, and the premium cap extension. A player who achieves this configuration can expect roughly 4-6x the offline income of an average player with the same nominal pet value. The gap widens further during events, when event-specific multipliers add another 2-3x on top of the base configuration.

The math is unforgiving, but it is also learnable. Every percentage point of happiness, every pet slot freed by trimming commons, and every additional login cycle translates directly into offline cash. Players who treat the offline system as a math problem rather than a passive waiting room consistently out-earn players who just collect and go. The formulas above are the foundation. The rest is discipline and daily execution.
