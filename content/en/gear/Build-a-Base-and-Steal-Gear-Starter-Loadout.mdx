The right starter gear loadout in Build a Base and Steal is the difference between a beginner who wins their first few raids and a beginner who loses them all. ## The Four Gear Categories

Build a Base and Steal divides gear into four categories: weapons, tools, support items, and consumables. Each category serves a different purpose in the raid, and a balanced loadout includes at least one item from each category. Beginners who focus exclusively on weapons often find themselves losing raids to better-equipped opponents who can breach faster, extract more loot, and escape more easily.

Weapons are the primary offensive tools and are used to break through walls and obstacles during a raid. They generate damage over time, and the damage rate determines how quickly you can breach a target base. A strong weapon is essential, but it is only one piece of the puzzle.

Tools are the utility items that complement your weapon. They include breaching charges, grappling hooks, scanners, and other items that help you navigate the base and overcome obstacles. Tools do not deal damage directly, but they enable your weapon to do its job by removing the barriers that would otherwise slow you down.

Support items are the defensive and mobility tools that help you survive during a raid. They include shields, healing items, escape tools, and similar items that keep you alive long enough to extract your loot. A loadout without support items is a loadout that relies entirely on speed, which is not a sustainable strategy.

Consumables are the one-use items that provide temporary boosts. They include damage boosts, speed boosts, and special abilities that can turn the tide of a raid. Consumables are expensive, and beginners should use them sparingly.

### The Recommended Starter Loadout

For a brand-new player, the recommended starter loadout includes the basic version of one weapon, one tool, one support item, and one consumable. The exact items depend on the current patch and the available stock in the shop, but the categories should be balanced.

The starter weapon should be the basic blaster, which is the cheapest weapon in the shop and provides consistent damage output. The blaster is not the most powerful weapon, but it is reliable, easy to use, and affordable. New players should save up for the upgraded blaster as their first major purchase.

The starter tool should be the basic breaching charge, which is a one-use item that quickly destroys a single wall. Breaching charges are essential for any raid, because every base has at least one wall that needs to come down before you can reach the loot. New players should always carry at least one breaching charge.

The starter support item should be the basic shield, which absorbs damage for a few seconds and can save your life in a close fight. Shields are not as flashy as weapons, but they are the difference between a raid that ends in extraction and a raid that ends in failure.

The starter consumable should be the basic damage boost, which provides a temporary increase to your weapon damage. Use it during the final breach of a tough base, where the extra damage can mean the difference between success and failure.

### Budgeting for Gear Upgrades

Gear upgrades in Build a Base and Steal are not cheap, and beginners should plan their purchases carefully. The most important rule is to upgrade one item at a time rather than spreading cash across multiple items. A player with one strong weapon and basic tools is more effective than a player with multiple weak items.

The upgrade priority is usually weapons first, tools second, support items third, and consumables last. Weapons have the biggest impact on raid outcomes, so they should be upgraded first. Tools are next, because they enable your weapon to do its job. Support items are third, because they keep you alive. Consumables are last, because they are one-use items that do not provide permanent value.

A reasonable upgrade budget is to spend 40 percent of your gear cash on weapons, 25 percent on tools, 25 percent on support items, and 10 percent on consumables. This split gives you a balanced loadout that can handle most raid scenarios, and it leaves room for occasional consumable use.

### Where to Buy Gear

Gear can be purchased from the in-game shop, the trading hub, or the event shop. The in-game shop is the most reliable source, but it has limited stock and rotates items regularly. The trading hub is a player-driven market where you can find rare gear that is no longer in the shop. The event shop is a limited-time source of exclusive gear that can only be obtained during events.

For beginners, the in-game shop is the best source. The prices are stable, the stock is reliable, and the items are guaranteed to work. The trading hub is more advanced and requires a good understanding of the market to avoid overpaying. The event shop is worth checking during events, because some event-exclusive gear items are very strong and can be obtained at a discount.

## Common Starter Loadout Mistakes

The most common mistake is buying the most expensive item in the shop before you have the cash to support a full loadout. A player who spends their entire reserve on the best weapon is left with no cash for tools, support items, or consumables, and they are easily defeated by a better-equipped opponent.

The second most common mistake is ignoring the support items. Many new players think that raiding is all about damage output, and they invest everything in weapons. The result is a loadout that can deal a lot of damage but cannot survive a counter-attack, and the player loses raids that they should have won.

The third most common mistake is using consumables too early. Damage boosts and other consumables are most valuable during the final moments of a difficult raid, when the extra power can make the difference. Using them at the start of every raid is wasteful and leaves you without consumables for the tough raids that actually need them.

## Upgrading Your Loadout Over Time

As you progress through the game, your loadout should evolve to match your goals. The starter loadout is fine for the first few weeks, but as you start attempting more difficult raids, you will need better gear. The upgrade path is usually a slow progression through the rarity tiers of each item, starting with the basic versions and ending with the legendary versions.

The legendary tier of each gear category is the end goal for most players. Legendary weapons deal 3x to 5x the damage of basic weapons, legendary tools provide permanent utility benefits, and legendary support items provide game-changing defensive abilities. Acquiring a full legendary loadout is a long-term goal that takes months of dedicated play, but it is the foundation of the end-game raiding experience.

The upgrade path should be flexible. Some players prefer to focus on weapons, because they enjoy the offensive playstyle. Other players prefer to focus on tools and support items, because they enjoy the tactical playstyle. The optimal loadout is the one that matches your personal playstyle, not the one that matches a generic template.
