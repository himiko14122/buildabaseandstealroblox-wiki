## The Six-Tier Rarity Structure

Build a Base and Steal uses a six-tier rarity structure for its pets, and each tier has its own probability of appearing on any given roll. The tiers, from most common to rarest, are Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary, and Mythic. Each tier comes with a higher income multiplier and a higher trading value, which is why the rarer tiers are so coveted. The drop rates below are based on the standard rolling machine during non-event periods.

Common pets make up roughly 50 to 55 percent of all rolls. They are the bread and butter of any collection, generating the base income rate and providing the foundation that every stronger collection is built on. Uncommon pets account for about 25 to 28 percent of rolls. They are noticeably more valuable than commons and form them meaningfully rare but not unobtainable. A player who rolls 100 times in a week will typically receive 12 to 15 rare pets, and a few of those will end up being worth real trading value. Epic pets drop at roughly 5 to 7 percent, meaning a player needs to roll around 15 to 20 times to expect one. Legendary pets drop at about 2 to 3 percent, and Mythic pets are the rarest of all, at less than 1 percent. The exact percentages can shift with patches, but the relative ordering is stable.

### How the Pity System Works

Because pure random rolls can produce long dry streaks, Build a Base and Steal includes a pity system that guarantees a rare-tier or-better pet after a certain number of rolls without one. The standard pity threshold is 50 rolls, meaning that if you perform 50 consecutive rolls without receiving at least a Rare pet, the system automatically gives you one on roll 51. This guarantee is not a hidden feature; it is a documented mechanic designed to keep players engaged and to prevent the feeling that the game is being unfair.

The pity system also includes tier-specific guarantees. After 100 rolls without an Epic, you are guaranteed an Epic. After 250 rolls without a Legendary, you are guaranteed a Legendary. These guarantees are independent; pity Rare pity is not any closer to their Epic pity.

This structure is important for budget planning because it means that the worst-case scenario for a rolling session is bounded. You cannot roll 200 times and receive zero rares; the pity system guarantees at least one Rare in the first 50 rolls, at least one Epic in the first 100, and at least one Legendary in the first 250. The cost of a guaranteed Legendary, at the standard roll price, gives you a hard upper bound on how much you might need to spend to obtain one.

### Expected Value and Variance

Every roll has an expected value based on the average pet you would receive over many rolls. The expected value of a single roll in Build a Base and Steal is roughly equivalent to a Common pet, with a small chance of being upgraded by the rarity tier you happen to hit. Over 100 rolls, the expected value is around 55 commons, 27 uncommons, 14 rares, 6 epics, 2 to 3 legendaries, and less than 1 mythic. These are averages, not promises; the actual outcome of any individual 100-roll session will deviate from these numbers, sometimes significantly.

The variance is what makes rolling exciting and dangerous. A player can roll 50 times and receive 20 commons, 20 uncommons, 5 rares, 1 epic, and zero legendaries, well below average. Both outcomes are normal, and neither indicates that the game is rigged. Variance is the price of admission to a probabilistic system, and understanding it is the difference between a player who rides out a cold streak calmly and a player who chases losses into bankruptcy.

The key statistical insight is that variance shrinks as the sample size grows. Over 1,000 rolls, your results will be very close to the expected distribution. Over 100 rolls, they will be moderately close. Over 10 rolls, they can be wildly different. This is why sessions that chase a single rare pet in 5 or 10 rolls are so unreliable, while a long-term rolling plan that spreads rolls across weeks and months is so consistent.

### Event-Boosted Drop Rates

During special events, Build a Base and Steal sometimes increases the drop rates for specific pets or specific rarity tiers. A typical event might double the chance of receiving an Epic, or boost a particular featured pet's drop rate by five times. These boosts are usually time-limited and stack with the regular pity system, meaning you can burn through for the affected pet or tier. If you have been saving for a Legendary, and the current event doubles the Legendary drop rate, that is the moment to spend your rolling budget. The expected value of each roll during a boost is significantly higher than during normal play, and the same budget will yield noticeably better results.

Be cautious, however, of event-exclusive traps. Some events introduce a "featured" pet that has a much lower drop rate than the headline boost suggests, or that requires a special currency that can only be obtained through the event itself. Always read the event details before spending, and calculate the actual expected value of an event roll, not just the advertised multiplier. A 5x boost on a pet that starts at 0.1 percent is still only 0.5 percent, which is not a great deal.
