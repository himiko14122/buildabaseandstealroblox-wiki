Cash farming in Build a Base and Steal is the art of converting time and effort into a sustainable income stream that funds your rolling, building, and raiding activities. The best farming strategies are not about grinding mindlessly. They are about identifying the most efficient activities, optimizing the loop between those activities, and leveraging offline income to keep the farm running even when you are not playing. This guide covers the major farming strategies, the activities that compose them, and the optimization techniques that turn a moderate time investment into a meaningful income stream.

## The Three Farming Loops

Build a Base and Steal has three main farming loops: the rolling loop, the raiding loop, and the trading loop. Each loop has different time requirements, risk profiles, and expected returns. The best farmers use all three loops in combination, switching between them based on the current state of the game and the available opportunities.

The rolling loop is the most passive. You spend cash on rolls, you receive pets, you place the pets in your base, and the pets generate income. The loop is simple but slow, because the cash you spend on rolls must be recouped from pet income before the loop becomes profitable. The rolling loop is the foundation of every other farming strategy, because the pets you acquire through rolling are the inputs to the other loops.

The raiding loop is more active. You spend time selecting targets, preparing your gear, and executing raids. The raids produce loot in the form of pets and cash, which you can use for further rolling or trading. The raiding loop is faster than the rolling loop, but it requires attention and skill, and it carries the risk of failed raids that cost you gear.

The trading loop is the most strategic. You buy low and sell high in the trading hub, profiting from the spread between what other players are willing to pay and what you paid to acquire the items. The trading loop is the highest-skill loop, but it also has the highest potential returns for players who understand the market.

### The Rolling Farm

The rolling farm is the most accessible farming strategy for new players. The setup is simple: accumulate cash through any means (login rewards, code rewards, offline income), spend it on rolls, and place the resulting pets in your base. The pets generate income that you can spend on more rolls, creating a self-sustaining loop.

The efficiency of the rolling farm depends on the rarity of the pets you acquire. A common pet generates 1 cash per second, which means it takes roughly 17 minutes for a common pet to recoup the cost of its roll. An uncommon pet generates 1.5 cash per second and recoups its cost in about 11 minutes. A rare pet recoups in about 5 minutes. A legendary recoups in about 2 minutes. The recoup time decreases with rarity, which means that the rolling farm is more efficient when you are acquiring higher-rarity pets.

The optimization of the rolling farm is in the storage management. Every pet you add to the farm increases the total income, but only if the pet contributes to a family bonus or has above-average rarity. Adding common pets that do not contribute to bonuses dilutes the income per slot, and a bloated collection of low-value pets is less efficient than a tight collection of high-value pets. Most experienced farmers focus on family completion over raw pet count.

### The Raiding Farm

The raiding farm is more active than the rolling farm but produces faster returns. The setup requires a modest investment in gear, which you can acquire through the rolling farm or through the trading loop. Once you have the gear, you can execute raids against other players' bases to extract pets and cash.

The efficiency of the raiding farm depends on your target selection and your success rate. A successful raid on a moderately defended base can produce 5,000 to 20,000 cash worth of loot, which is much faster than the rolling farm. A failed raid costs you the gear you used, which can set you back significantly if you are using high-tier gear.

The optimization of the raiding farm is in the target selection. The most profitable targets are bases with weak defenses and high-value loot, which are usually bases owned by newer or less experienced players. The least profitable targets are heavily defended bases with low-value loot, which are usually bases owned by veteran players who use the bases as decoys. The farmer who can quickly distinguish between the two types of targets will have a much higher profit per raid.

A second optimization is the raid energy management. The raid energy regenerates over time, and the rate depends on the current event and your Premium Pass status. The most efficient farmers use their raid energy as soon as it regenerates, because unused raid energy is wasted potential. The exception is during events that boost raid rewards, when it can be worth saving the energy for the event window.

### The Trading Farm

The trading farm is the highest-skill farming strategy. The setup requires an initial investment in tradeable items, which you can acquire through the rolling farm or the raiding farm. Once you have the items, you can list them in the trading hub and profit from the market spread.

The efficiency of the trading farm depends on your market knowledge and your timing. A trader who buys a pet when its price is low and sells it when its price is high can make a significant profit, often 50 to 200 percent of the purchase price. A trader who buys at the wrong time can lose money, because the trading hub charges a transaction fee and the market can move against you.

The optimization of the trading farm is in the market analysis. The most profitable traders track the prices of the most-traded items, identify the patterns of price movement around events and updates, and use that information to time their buys and sells. The market analysis is a skill that develops over time, and new traders should start with small positions before risking larger amounts.

A second optimization is the item selection. Not every item is profitable to trade. The most profitable items are those that have high demand and limited supply, like newly released legendary pets or event-exclusive items. The least profitable items are those that are abundant and have low demand, like common pets that everyone rolls in large quantities.

## Combining the Loops

The most efficient farmers use all three loops in combination. The rolling farm provides the baseline income and the pet collection that feeds the trading farm. The raiding farm provides the high-value items that are too rare to roll for reliably. The trading farm converts the items acquired through rolling and raiding into cash that funds further rolling and raiding.

The combination is most efficient when the loops are synchronized. For example, during an event with boosted rolling rates, the farmer rolls aggressively to acquire event-exclusive pets, which are then sold through the trading farm when prices peak. Between events, the farmer focuses on raiding to acquire the high-value pets that are too rare to roll for, which are also sold through the trading farm.

The synchronization requires planning, and the most successful farmers plan their week around the event calendar. They know which events are coming, they know which items will spike in price, and they position their farms accordingly. The planning is the difference between a farmer who makes a steady profit and a farmer who occasionally gets lucky.

## Long-Term Farm Sustainability

The most important aspect of farming is long-term sustainability. A farm that produces a short-term spike but depletes your resources is not sustainable. A farm that produces a steady, modest income over months is the foundation of long-term success.

Sustainability is built on three principles: diversification, conservation, and reinvestment. Diversification means spreading your farming across multiple loops, so that a downturn in one loop does not wipe out your income. Conservation means avoiding the all-in approach, so that you always have resources to keep the farm running. Reinvestment means putting a portion of your farming profits back into the farm, so that the farm grows over time.

The principles sound simple, but they require discipline, especially when you are tempted by a hot opportunity or a tempting trade. The farmers who follow the principles for months and years build farms that produce consistent, predictable income. The farmers who chase every opportunity end up with depleted resources and a farm that no longer produces.
