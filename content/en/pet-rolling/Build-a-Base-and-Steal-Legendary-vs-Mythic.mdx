Legendary and mythic pets are the apex of the Build a Base and Steal collection hierarchy, and the decision of which tier to focus your rolling budget on is one of the most important strategic choices in the game. The two tiers have very different rolling costs, income rates, collection bonuses, and trading values, and the right choice depends on your account maturity, your budget, and your long-term goals. This guide compares the two tiers across every meaningful dimension and gives you a framework for deciding which tier to prioritize.

## The Rolling Cost Difference

The most immediate difference between legendary and mythic pets is the rolling cost. The base rolling price is the same for both tiers, but the pity thresholds and the practical rolling volume are very different. The legendary pity threshold is 150 rolls, while the mythical pity threshold is 300 rolls. A player who is targeting mythics through pity alone needs to roll roughly twice as many times as a player targeting legendaries.

The actual rolling cost in cash is also different, because the higher pity threshold means more total rolls are required on average. The expected number of rolls to get a legendary is roughly 100 (because most players will hit a legendary organically before reaching pity). The expected number of rolls to get a mythical is roughly 250 (because mythics are rarer, and many players will hit pity). At 100 cash per roll, the expected rolling cost is 10,000 cash for a legendary and 25,000 cash for a mythical.

The cash difference is significant, and it is the main reason that most players should prioritize legendaries over mythics during the mid-game. The income from a single legendary is roughly 40 percent of the income from a single mythical, but the rolling cost is 60 percent lower, which makes the legendary the better per-roll investment for most budgets.

### The Income Rate Difference

The income rate difference between legendary and mythical pets is meaningful but not as large as the rarity might suggest. A legendary pet generates 8 cash per second at base level, while a mythical pet generates 20 cash per second. The mythical is 2.5 times the legendary, but the rolling cost is roughly 2.5 times as well, so the per-cash-invested income is similar.

The collection bonuses are where the two tiers diverge more clearly. Legendary collection bonuses tend to be moderate, usually providing 1.2x to 1.5x income multipliers to the family. Mythical collection bonuses tend to be much larger, often providing 2x to 3x income multipliers. A complete mythical collection is significantly more valuable than a complete legendary collection, but the cost of completing a mythical collection is also significantly higher.

The income difference also depends on the pet level. Both legendary and mythical pets can be leveled up, and the level multiplier applies to both. A level 50 legendary and a level 50 mythical both have the same level multiplier, which means the relative income difference stays roughly the same regardless of level.

### The Trading Value Difference

Mythical pets are significantly more valuable in the trading hub than legendary pets, both because they are rarer and because they have higher demand. A single mythical pet can sell for 50,000 to 200,000 cash in the trading hub, depending on the specific pet and the current market. A single legendary pet usually sells for 5,000 to 20,000 cash. The mythical is roughly 5 to 10 times the trading value of a legendary.

The higher trading value of mythics makes them attractive to players who want to convert their pet collection into cash. The trading value is also more volatile for mythics, because the market is thinner and individual sales can move the price significantly. A player who holds a mythical for a long time is exposed to the risk of price drops, but a player who sells at the right time can realize a substantial profit.

The trading value difference is also affected by events. During events that feature specific mythical pets, the prices of those pets spike, while the prices of non-featured mythics may drop. The volatility around events is another reason that the trading value of mythics is more variable than legendaries.

### The Practical Acquisition Difference

The practical experience of acquiring a legendary is very different from acquiring a mythical. Legendary pets drop frequently enough that most players will roll several within a few weeks of active play. The lucky player rolls one within their first 50 rolls. The unlucky player hits the 150 pity threshold and gets a guaranteed legendary.

Mythical pets are much rarer in practice. Most players will roll for weeks or months before receiving a mythical, and many will hit the 300 pity threshold to get their first one. The pity threshold is the most reliable way to acquire a mythical, which is why the save-and-burst strategy is so common for players targeting mythics.

The acquisition effort also affects how you should treat the pets. A legendary that you rolled in your first 50 rolls feels easy to obtain, and you may treat it more casually. A mythical that you saved for months to obtain feels precious, and you are more likely to invest in leveling it, protecting it, and using it as the centerpiece of your base. The psychological difference is real and affects how players manage their collection.

## Which Tier to Chase

The right tier to chase depends on your account state and your goals. For most players, the recommendation is to chase legendaries during the early to mid-game, and only switch to mythics once the legendary collection is complete and the base defenses are established.

Chasing legendaries first is the right call because:
- The per-roll investment is better, which means more legendaries for the same cash.


- The collection bonuses from complete legendary sets are achievable within a few months of active play.


- The trading value of legendaries is meaningful, so excess legendaries can be sold to fund other activities.


- The risk of over-investing in mythics is lower when you have a strong legendary foundation.

Switching to mythics is the right call once:
- Your legendary collection is complete and your family bonuses are active.


- Your base defenses are strong enough to protect a mythical pet.


- You have a cash reserve large enough to fund a 300-roll pity session without breaking your budget.


- You understand the trading market well enough to time your mythical sales.

## The Hybrid Strategy

The most efficient strategy is the hybrid approach, where you pursue legendaries as your main focus and treat mythics as occasional bonuses. The hybrid approach is sustainable because it does not require massive cash reserves, and it produces a balanced collection that includes both tiers.

The hybrid approach works by rolling your normal rolling budget (the 50% of the 50/30/20 split) on the standard rolling machine, which produces legendaries regularly and produces mythics occasionally. When you hit a mythical organically, you treat it as a windfall and either keep it for your collection or sell it for a substantial profit. When you do not hit a mythical, you have a strong legendary collection that generates meaningful income.

The hybrid approach is less efficient for players who want to complete the mythical collection quickly, but it is more efficient for players who want to build a strong overall account. The mythical collection is the long-term goal, not the immediate priority, and the hybrid approach builds the foundation that makes the long-term goal achievable.
