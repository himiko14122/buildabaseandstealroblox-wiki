Limited-time events in Build a Base and Steal are the most concentrated source of valuable pets in the entire game. Boosted drop rates, exclusive pets, event currency shops, and community-wide goals all combine to make event periods the highest-value rolling opportunities of the year. But events are also when budget discipline matters most, because the pressure to spend is intense and the consequences of poor decisions are amplified. This guide covers the event types, the optimal spending strategies, and the decision framework that and its own optimal strategy. The most common type is the drop rate boost, where the probability of receiving one or more specific rarity tiers is increased for the event duration. These events are the simplest to evaluate: if the boost applies to a tier you are chasing, spend your rolling budget during the event. If not, save the budget for the next event.

The second type is the featured pet event, where a specific pet has its drop rate dramatically increased, sometimes by 5x or more. Featured pet events are the most exciting but also the most variable. If you want the featured pet, the boost makes this the best time to roll for it. If you do not want the pet, the boost is irrelevant to you, and you should not change your rolling plan.

The third type is the event currency event, where rolling and other activities grant a special currency that can be spent in a limited-time shop. These events reward consistent play and offer exclusive pets that cannot be obtained any other way. ## Pre-Event Planning

The most successful event players in Build a Base and Steal start planning for events one to two weeks in advance. The planning phase has three components: deciding how much cash to save, deciding which event type to prioritize, and deciding which pets to target.

Cash saving is the most mechanical component. Most event players aim to have at least 30 to 50 percent more cash on hand at the start of an event than they would during normal play. This reserve is what allows them to take advantage of mid-event opportunities, like a surprise flash boost or a community milestone that unlocks a personal reward. Players who arrive at events with empty reserves can only spend what they earn during the event, which usually is not enough to capitalize on the best opportunities.

Prioritizing event types is more strategic. If you have a long wishlist of pets and limited cash, you need to decide which events matter most to you. A player who is missing the featured pet of next week's entirely.

Targeting specific pets within an event is the most nuanced decision. Most events feature a "headline" pet that everyone talks about, but they also feature several "hidden" pets that are easier to obtain and just as valuable. A pro event player does not chase only the headline pet; they spread their rolls across all the boosted pets and end up with a more diverse haul. The headline pet is one target among several, not the only goal.

### Mid-Event Decision Making

The middle of an event is when the most expensive decisions are made. The community is excited, the chat is buzzing about lucky rolls, and the temptation to spend your reserve is intense. The mid-event decisions that matter most are whether to spend the reserve, whether to liquidate duplicates, and whether to participate in the event's secondary activities.

Spending the reserve is a decision that should be made based on data, not feelings. If you are 70 percent through the event and you have hit your main targets, do not spend the reserve chasing a long-shot pet. If you are 70 percent through the event and you have not hit your main targets, evaluate whether the reserve is likely to change your outcome. Sometimes you would need to spend to reach your goal.

Liquidating duplicates is usually a good idea during events, because the trading market is more active and prices are higher. A duplicate Epic that you would normally hold for a future trade might be worth selling during an event to fund a roll for the featured pet. The decision to sell should be based on the expected value of the cash versus the expected value of holding the duplicate for a future trade.

Participating in secondary activities (like mini-games, daily quests, or community challenges) is a way to earn event currency without spending cash. These activities are usually worth the time investment, because the currency-to-cash ratio is favorable and the rewards are event-exclusive. The only time to skip them is when the time cost is so high that it interferes with your main rolling plan.

### Post-Event Wrap Up

The days after an event are just as important as the days during it. Post-event, you should review your results, log your spending, and adjust your plans for the next event. The review phase is where long-term improvement happens, because it is where you identify what worked and what did not.

## Common Event Mistakes

The most expensive event mistake in Build a Base and Steal is the FOMO spend, where a player spends their entire reserve in the first hours of an event because they are afraid of missing out on the early bonuses. FOMO spends usually produce mediocre results, because the player has not yet developed a clear sense of what they are chasing. The fix is to set a per-day event budget, stick to it, and reassess at the end of each day based on what you have actually received.

The second most expensive mistake is the headline chase, where a player spends everything on the featured pet and ignores the other boosted pets. The featured pet is usually a small fraction of the total value available during exclusively on rolling during events and forget to spend their event currency in the limited-time shop. The shop usually offers exclusive pets and items that are unavailable after the event ends, and currency that is not spent is wasted. Always clear the shop, or at least buy the most valuable items, before the event ends.

Event play in Build a Base and Steal is a skill that improves with practice. Your first few events will be a learning experience, and that is fine.
