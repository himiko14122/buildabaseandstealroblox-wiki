Raiding is the offensive counterpart to base defense, and it is the part of Build a Base and Steal that most new players find confusing. The mechanics are simple on the surface but layered in practice, and a single bad raid can cost you more gear than the pets you steal Raid?

To start a raid in Build a Base and Steal, you need to be in a public server and have at least one piece of raiding gear equipped. Walk up to another player's base, equip your gear, and click the raid icon. The game will prompt you to confirm, and once you confirm, you have a 60-second window to break in, steal up to three pets, and extract back to the lobby.

Raids cost gear durability, and each piece of gear has a limited number of uses before it breaks. A hammer typically lasts 5 to 8 raids, a lockpick lasts 10 to 15, and a speed boost is single-use. The cost of a raid is the gear durability consumed, plus any pets you lose if the defender retaliates while you are in their base.

### What Gear Do I Need?

The minimum gear to start a raid is a hammer, which is the basic wall-breaking tool. Without a hammer, you cannot break through any wall, and your raid will be limited to whatever pets you can pick up by reaching through gaps. Most players also bring a lockpick, which lets them open locked doors faster, and a speed boost, which lets them escape if the raid goes wrong.

## How Do I Choose a Target?

Target selection is the single most important raiding skill. A good target is a base that is lightly defended, has a valuable pet collection, and is owned by a player who is not currently online. A bad target is a heavily defended base with mid-tier pets, owned by a player who will log on and retaliate while you are inside.

The best way to scout targets is to walk the public server and look at every base you pass. Most players leave their base visible, and you can see the wall count, the trap count, and the visible pets without entering the base. Bases with 5 or fewer walls and no turrets are easy targets. Bases with 20+ walls and multiple turrets are targets to avoid unless you have a coordinated team and premium gear.

Coordinate the time and server in advance so that you are not interrupted by strangers.

## What Happens If I Die During a Raid?

If you die during a raid, you respawn in the lobby with no pets and half of your gear durability consumed. You can re-enter the raid, but the defender will have been alerted, and the pet locations may have changed. Most players use death as a signal to retreat, not to re-engage, because the cost of re-entering usually exceeds the expected value of the pets.

The exception is when the raid is in the final stage and you are one wall away from the pets. In that case, re-engaging can be worth it, especially if you have a backup set of gear. Most experienced players carry a "raid kit" with a primary set of gear and a backup set, so that they can recover from a death without ending the raid.

### Can I Get Banned for even if you raid the same target repeatedly. The only raiding-related offenses are exploits (using third-party tools to break walls instantly) and griefing (raiding the same target over and over with no intent to extract pets), and even these result in warnings before any kind of ban.

The line between aggressive play and griefing is fuzzy, but the developers generally interpret it based on the attacker's behavior pattern. Raiding a target once per day is fine. Raiding the same target 20 times in an hour will trigger a griefing warning, and persistent offenders will be temporarily banned from the raid system. Most players never encounter this, because the raid system has natural limits (gear durability, server population, time) that prevent truly excessive play.

## How Many Pets Can I Steal Per Raid?

The maximum is three pets per raid. This is a hard cap, and it does not change with Premium Pass or any other upgrade. The cap exists to keep raids from being too punishing on the defender side, and it ensures that even a successful raid only costs the defender a small fraction of their collection.

The three pets you steal have to walk to the pet, click on it, and have it added to your extraction inventory. The extraction inventory holds up to 3 pets; if you pick up a 4th, the oldest one is automatically dropped.

### Is Raiding Worth the Time?

For new players, raiding is a mixed bag. The gear cost is significant relative to your income, and the pets you can steal are usually low-tier. For mid-game players, raiding becomes a reliable source of uncommon and rare pets, especially if you are targeting bases that are slightly weaker than yours. For end-game players, raiding is one of the primary ways to acquire specific pets for family completion, because the trading market for top-tier pets is thin and the only practical source is a successful raid.

The time cost of a raid is typically 5 to 15 minutes, including the time to find a target, the raid itself, and the extraction. Compared to rolling, which is essentially instant, raiding is a bigger time investment. The trade-off is that each raid can produce 1 to 3 pets for the cost of a few rolls, which is a better return per unit of time if you are efficient at target selection.
