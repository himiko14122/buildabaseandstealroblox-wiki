Pet storage in Build a Base and Steal is not just about finding a place to keep your pets. It is about organizing your collection to maximize income, minimize raid risk, and simplify daily management. Beginners who treat storage as an afterthought end up with cluttered, disorganized bases that lose pets to raids and waste cash on unnecessary protections. This guide teaches the storage principles that experienced players use to keep their collections safe and productive.

## The Three-Tier System

Tier one holds your legendary and epic pets, protected by your strongest defenses. Tier two holds your mid-rarity pets, protected by standard defenses. Tier three holds your common pets, often placed as buffers in vulnerable positions. This system works because it matches the protection level to the pet value, ensuring that your most valuable assets get the strongest protection while your common pets absorb raid attempts.

The three-tier system is not just about placement. It is also about mental clarity. When you know exactly which tier each pet belongs to, you can make faster decisions about upgrades, trades, and base improvements. Players without a tier system constantly second-guess their pet placement and end up wasting time on minor optimizations that do not matter.

### Defining Your Tiers

The exact tier boundaries depend on your playstyle, but a common beginner split is simple. Tier one: legendary and epic pets. Tier two: rare pets. Tier three: everything else. As you progress, you may want to refine this split. For example, you might decide that a specific rare pet deserves tier-one treatment because of its unique income rate, or that a particular epic is overvalued and can be downgraded to tier two. The flexibility of the tier system is part of what makes it powerful.

When defining your tiers, also consider income streak. The tier system should reflect the actual value of each pet to your progression, not just the rarity label.

## Placement Geography

The physical placement of your pets within your base is just as important as the tier they belong to. In Build a Base and Steal, the center of your base is the most protected location, and the perimeter is the most exposed. The standard placement rule is: tier one pets in the center, tier two pets in the middle ring, tier three pets near the perimeter. Raiders who breach the outer wall should encounter commons first, then rares, and only the most dedicated attackers will reach your epics and legendaries.

Placement geography also affects your offline income. Pets near the edge of your base are easier to steal, but the income they generate is the same regardless of location. The trade-off is clear: high-value pets in the most protected position, low-value pets where they are most likely to be lost. If a raid takes your commons, you lose almost nothing. If a raid takes your legendaries, you lose hours of progression.

### The Decoy Strategy

Some experienced players use the perimeter placement strategically. They place a few low-tier pets in obvious locations to draw raiders' attention, while hiding their real treasures in unexpected places inside the base. The decoy strategy works because most raiders are looking for fast wins. If they find a few-cost defense that pays off over many raids.

The risk of the decoy strategy is that experienced raiders will recognize it and ignore the decoys, focusing their search on the protected core. Use decoys against casual raiders, but do not rely on them against experienced attackers. A truly well-designed base looks empty at first glance, with the high-value pets hidden in secondary chambers or behind false walls.

## Storage Upgrades

Build a Base and Steal offers storage upgrades that increase the number of pets you can keep in your base at once. These upgrades cost cash, but they pay for themselves quickly because each additional pet slot increases your offline income rate. Beginners should prioritize storage upgrades over wall upgrades in the early game, because the income benefit compounds while the wall benefit only matters during raids.

The optimal time to upgrade storage is when you are about to roll for new pets but have no space for them. Wasting a high-rarity pet because your storage is full is one of the most expensive beginner mistakes. Before you ever approach the rolling machine, make sure you have at least two empty storage slots. This single habit eliminates a whole class of painful losses.

### Storage Types and Off-Site Safekeeping

Off-site safekeeping options exist outside your base. These storage types are more secure than base storage but usually have limitations, like reduced income rates or transfer costs. Use external storage for your absolute most valuable pets that you cannot bear to lose, and accept the reduced income as a security premium.

External storage also helps with the decoy strategy. If you have legendary pets in external storage, you can place commons in your base as decoys without worrying about losing anything truly valuable. The external storage acts as insurance for your most prized possessions, freeing you to take more risks in your base design.

## Pet Organization Tools

Keeping track of your pet collection is harder than it sounds. By the time you have thirty pets, you may not remember the income rate of each one or which tier it belongs to. Build a Base and Steal offers in-game tools to help with this, but many players find external tracking more reliable. A simple spreadsheet with pet name, rarity, income rate, and tier column is enough to keep your collection organized.

The benefit of external tracking is that it survives who have experienced a server reset know how valuable external records are, and the five minutes it takes to set up a spreadsheet pays for itself the first time the reset happens.

### Collection Value Calculation

For each pet in your storage, calculate its value to your economy. The simplest formula is income rate times expected lifetime, which gives you a rough estimate of how much cash the pet will generate before it is replaced or lost. Pets with high lifetime value deserve tier-one protection. Pets with low lifetime value can afford to be in tier three. The calculation does not need to be exact, but doing it forces you to think about each pet as an economic asset rather than a collectible.

Players who do this calculation often discover that some of their legendary pets are less valuable than a few of their rare pets, because the legendaries have lower income rates or because they are sitting in storage doing nothing. Once you know the actual value of each pet, you can make smarter decisions about which pets to keep, which to trade, and which to use as decoys.

## The Trading Connection

Pet storage in Build a Base and Steal intersects with the trading system. Some players keep large collections with the explicit to trade from. If you decide to focus on trading, your storage strategy should be different from the standard three-tier system.

Trader storage usually involves keeping a separate inventory of trade-stock pets, with a clear distinction between pets you use for income and pets you hold for trading. The trade-stock inventory does not need heavy protection because the pets are not in active use, but it should be organized for quick access. The income-generating inventory, by contrast, gets the full three-tier protection.

### Storage Discipline

The final storage principle is discipline. Once you have a system in place, stick to it. Do not move pets around on impulse. Do not upgrade storage unless you actually need the slots. Do not add a new tier unless your collection has clearly outgrown the current one. The storage system is only as good as your commitment to it, and the most common reason players end up with chaotic storage is that they keep changing the rules every session.

Build a Base and Steal rewards organized players. The three-tier system, the placement geography, the storage upgrades, and the external tracking all add up to a small but consistent advantage that compounds over weeks. Players with is invisible in any single session, but it becomes enormous over a month.
