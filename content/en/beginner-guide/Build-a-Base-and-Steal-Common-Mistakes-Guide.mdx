## Mistake One: Spending All Cash on the First Roll

The most common beginner mistake is also the most damaging. A new player joins a server, sees the rolling machine, and spends every unit of starting cash in one sitting. The result is predictable: a handful of low-rarity pets, zero defenses, and a base that any raider can breach in seconds. The player logs off thinking they have made progress, returns to find an empty plot, and wonders what went wrong.

The fix is mechanical. Before you ever approach the rolling machine, decide on a session budget. The budget should follow the 50/30/20 rule: 50% to rolling, 30% to defense, 20% to reserve. If you start with 1,000 cash, that means no more than 500 on rolls, no more than 300 on walls, and at least 200 held back. The reserve is non-negotiable, even if it feels wasteful in the moment.

## Mistake Two: Building Without a Plan

The second most common mistake is the random base. A player opens the build menu, places walls wherever seems right, and ends up with a base that has no logic, no chokepoints, and no protected core. When a raid comes, the random base falls template works for most beginners. Place your highest-value pets in a central chamber. Surround the chamber with reinforced walls. Add a single controlled entrance with one or two traps. This layout can be built in under fifteen minutes, costs less than a full random base, and deters the vast majority of early-game raids. Stick with this template until you have enough experience to design your own.

### Mistake Three: Ignoring Offline Income

Many beginners play Build a Base and Steal as if it were a real-time game. They log in, do some rolling, log out, and never think about offline accumulation. They miss the single most powerful economic system in the game. The fix is to plan your sessions around offline income. Log out at strategic moments, plan for long breaks when you know you cannot play, and design your base to maximize offline collection rather than just in-session activity.

## Mistake Four: Chasing Rare Pets Too Early

The legendary chase is a trap. Beginners see the legendary tier in the rolling menu and convince themselves that they need a legendary pet to succeed. They spend hours and most of their cash chasing, not an early-game goal. Legendary pets are a late-game goal, not a mid-game goal.

The fix is to reframe your rolling goals. In your first week, your goal is not to roll a legendary. Your goal is to roll at least twenty pets of any rarity, build a base that protects them, and generate reliable offline income. If a legendary drops naturally, great. If not, you are still on track. The legendary will come later, when you have the cash and the base to support it.

### Mistake Five: Raiding Without Gear

The raid portal is one of the first things new players see, and many of them click it immediately. They enter a raid with no gear, no plan, and no understanding of the target base. They die in the first trap, lose any gear they happened to have, and learn nothing useful. The mistake is not the impulse to raid; the impulse is healthy. The mistake is raiding without preparation.

The fix is to acquire at least basic gear before your first real raid. Even the cheapest gear dramatically improves your success rate, and the difference between a successful raid and a failed raid is often just the cost of a starter hammer. Save up, buy the gear, and only then attempt the raid. A successful first raid teaches you you log out matters enormously in Build a Base and Steal. New players often log out in the middle of a roll, or right after a raid, or while their base is half-built. All of these are mistakes. The optimal logout is when your base is complete, your pets are placed, your cash is in a stable position, and offline accumulation is about to begin.

The fix is to treat the logout as a planned action, not an afterthought. Build your base first, then think about logging out. Make sure your pets are inside the walls, your walls are complete, and your cash reserve is intact. Only when all four conditions are met should you actually log out. This single habit dramatically improves your offline income and reduces the risk of returning to a damaged base.

### Mistake Seven: Decorative Over Function

Some beginners spend hours on decorative elements: custom wall patterns, themed color schemes, elaborate entrance designs. Decoration is fun, but in the early game it is a cash sink that provides no defensive value. A pretty base that falls to the first raid is worse than an ugly base that survives. The fix is to prioritize function over form until your base is genuinely defensible, then add decoration once the structure is solid.

## Mistake Eight: Trusting Strangers

The Build a Base and Steal community is mostly friendly, but the game is still a competitive environment. Some players will offer you trades that look good but are actually terrible. Others will offer to join your base as an ally and then raid you. The fix is to treat every interaction with another player as a potential risk, at least until you have built up enough trust.

Concretely, never give another player access to your base. Never trade your rarest pet for a stack of commons without verifying the values. Never accept a deal that sounds too good to be true. These are not paranoid rules; they are the standard rules of competitive multiplayer games, and they apply to Build a Base and Steal as much as anywhere else.

### Mistake Nine: Skipping the Codes

Codes are free rewards, and skipping them is leaving money on the table. Beginners often miss codes because they do not know where to look, or because they assume codes are not worth their time. The fix is to bookmark the codes page, check it at the start of every session, and redeem every active code immediately. Even a small code is worth more than the time it takes to type it in.

The item signals a recent balance change toward defense. A code for a rolling bonus signals a content update with new pets. Reading the codes page regularly keeps you informed about the meta.

## Mistake Ten: Playing Alone

The final mistake is the loner mistake. Build a Base and Steal is a multiplayer game, and players who try to play it entirely solo miss out on trades, alliances, information, and community events. The fix is to engage with the community early. Join the Discord, follow the subreddit, watch the YouTube content, and reach out to other players you encounter in-game. Most players are happy to share tips with newbies, and the few minutes you spend asking for advice can save you hours of trial and error.

### The Mistake Recovery Plan

If you have already made several of these mistakes, do not despair. The game is forgiving, especially in the first week. Pick the most damaging mistake you have made, fix it, and move on. Trying to fix everything at once usually leads to panic rebuilding, which is its own mistake. The recovery plan is sequential: stabilize your cash, stabilize your base, stabilize your pet roster, and then optimize. By the time you finish the sequence, most of your beginner mistakes will be behind you.

Build a Base and Steal rewards players who learn from their mistakes. The mistakes on this list are common back to it when you catch yourself slipping, and you will progress faster than 90% of new players.
