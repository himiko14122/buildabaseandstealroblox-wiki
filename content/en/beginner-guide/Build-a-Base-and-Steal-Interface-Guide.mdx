The Build a Base and Steal interface packs a remarkable amount of functionality into a relatively small set of menus, and beginners who do not learn the interface early spend the first week fumbling through screens instead of progressing. This guide walks through every major interface element, explains what each one does, and shows you the navigation patterns that experienced players use to move through the game efficiently. By the end, you will know where every important button is, what every menu shows, and how to chain interface actions together to save time on every play session.

## The Main HUD

The main heads-up display sits at the top or sides of the screen and shows your most important real-time information. The cash counter is always visible and updates in real time as your pets generate income. The storage indicator shows how many pets you own relative to your storage cap, often as a small icon with a number. The energy or stamina bar appears in some game modes and limits how often you can perform certain actions like raiding or rolling. The daily reward timer tells you when your next login reward is available.

The main HUD also includes quick-access buttons for the most common actions: the rolling machine button, the build menu button, the gear shop button, and the raid portal button. On mobile, these buttons appear as icons in a toolbar at the bottom. On PC, they appear as small icons in the corner of the screen. Clicking any of these buttons opens the corresponding menu or teleports you to the corresponding location in the game world.

### The Pet Collection Menu

The pet collection menu is where you view every pet you own, organized by rarity and family. The menu shows each pet's name, rarity, level, income rate, and family bonus contribution. Clicking a pet opens a detail view that shows the pet's full stats, any equipped items, and the options to level it up, sell it, or trade it. The collection menu is also where you can apply collection filters to find specific pets quickly, like filtering for "all legendary" or "all pets in the Puppy Pack family."

The pet collection menu is the most important menu in the game for long-term planning. Beginners use it to see what they have rolled. Intermediate players use it to track family completion progress. Advanced players use it to identify which pets are contributing the most income and which pets are candidates for recycling. The detail view is also where you can apply upgrade tokens to level up a pet, which is a major income boost that beginners often overlook.

### The Build Menu

The build menu is where you place walls, traps, obstacles, and decorative elements. The menu is organized by category: walls, traps, floors, decorations, and special items. Each category has a submenu that shows the available items, their costs, and their effects. Walls are listed by tier, from basic wood walls up to reinforced alloy walls. Traps are listed by type, from simple spike traps up to multi-stage trap systems. Floors and decorations are listed by visual style and have no gameplay effect.

The build menu also shows a placement preview when you select an item, which lets you see where the wall or trap will go before you commit cash. This is essential for efficient base design, because placing walls that block your own access is a common beginner mistake. The placement preview is color-coded: green means the placement is valid, red means the placement is invalid, and yellow means the placement is valid but blocks something else.

### The Gear Shop

The gear shop is where you purchase weapons, tools, support items, and consumables for raiding. The shop is organized by category, and each category shows the available items with their cost, stats, and a brief description. The shop rotates its stock periodically, and some items are limited to specific events or to players who have reached certain progression milestones. The shop also shows a recommended loadout for your current level, which is a useful starting point for beginners who do not yet know what to buy.

The gear shop is the second-largest cash sink in Build a Base and Steal after rolling, and it is where most of your long-term cash goes once you have a stable base. The recommended loadout changes as you progress, so check it whenever you level up, because the recommendation often points to a specific item that is significantly better than what you are currently using. The shop also has a trade-in option that lets you recycle old gear for a partial cash refund, which is useful when you are upgrading.

### The Raid Portal

The raid portal is the entry point to the raid system. Clicking the portal opens the raid interface, which shows the list of nearby bases, the list of your recent raids, and the list of bases that have recently raided you. The interface also shows your current raid energy or stamina, the cooldown before you can raid again, and the rewards available for completing daily raid missions. Selecting a base from the list opens the raid prep screen, which lets you choose your loadout and review the target before launching.

The raid interface is the most complex menu in the game, and beginners should expect to spend time learning it. The list of nearby bases updates in real time as bases are built, abandoned, and raided. The raid prep screen shows a rough preview of the target base's defenses, which is useful for planning your approach. The cooldown timer is critical, because exceeding your raid energy results in a longer wait before you can raid again, which is annoying during events.

## The Trading Hub

The trading hub is the central marketplace where players can buy and sell pets, gear, and other items. The hub shows current listings, recent transactions, and price trends for the most-traded items. The trading interface is straightforward: you list an item for sale, set your price, and wait for a buyer. Alternatively, you browse the listings and buy items that other players have put up for sale. The hub charges a small transaction fee, which is deducted from the seller's proceeds.

The trading hub is essential for players who want to optimize their collection without grinding every pet themselves. It is also the place where collection bonuses are easiest to complete, because you can buy the specific pets you are missing rather than waiting for them to drop. The hub is most active during events, when prices fluctuate and rare pets appear briefly on the market. Smart players buy during lulls and sell during events, which is the basic trading strategy that experienced players use.

## Settings and Account Menus

The settings menu is where you configure the game's options, including graphics quality, sound, notifications, and accessibility settings. The settings also include the account management options, which let you link your account to other services, manage your privacy, and view your purchase history. The settings menu is rarely needed once you have configured it, but it is worth visiting once to make sure your preferences are set.

The account menu includes your player profile, your progression stats, your transaction history, and the links to the support and help resources. The profile shows your current level, your total cash earned, your total pets collected, and your leaderboard rank. The progression stats are a useful self-check for tracking your long-term growth, and the leaderboard rank is the most competitive number in the game, because it is visible to other players and determines your matchmaking pool.

## Navigation Tips for New Players

The first navigation tip is to learn the keyboard shortcuts (on PC) or the touch gestures (on mobile). The most useful shortcut opens the build menu, because you will be accessing it constantly. The second most useful opens the pet collection, because you will check it after every rolling session. The third most useful opens the raid portal, because you will visit it multiple times per session. Memorize these three and you will save minutes of clicking every session.

The second tip is to use the minimap, which appears in the corner of the screen on most platforms. The minimap shows your base, nearby bases, the rolling machine, the gear shop, and the raid portal. When you are looking for a specific location, the minimap is faster than the HUD buttons, and it gives you a sense of the spatial layout of the server. Experienced players glance at the minimap every few seconds, and it becomes second nature after a few sessions.

The third tip is to bookmark the menus you visit most. On PC, most menus can be pinned to a quick-access bar. On mobile, the most useful menus can be added to a favorites list that appears at the top of the menu screen. The bookmark system is small, but it adds up to significant time savings over weeks of play.
