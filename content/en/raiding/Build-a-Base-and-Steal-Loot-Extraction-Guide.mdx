Loot extraction is the second half of a successful raid. Breaching gets you into the base, but extraction gets the valuables out. Most new raiders focus exclusively on the breach and treat extraction as an afterthought, which leads to lost loot, captured pets, and raids that turn profitable raids into net losses. This guide explains the mechanics of loot extraction, the prioritization framework, the secure extraction techniques, and the post-extraction processing that turns a successful raid into a successful outcome.

## The Extraction Window

Every raid in Build a Base and Steal has an extraction window. The window begins the moment you breach the outer wall and ends when you either exit the base or are neutralized. The length of the window depends on the type of raid: full of the raid. You are inside the base, the defender may be responding, and the clock is running. The choices you make during the window determine whether the raid is profitable. Hesitation, distraction, and poor prioritization are the most common causes of failed extractions, even when the breach was successful.

## Loot Prioritization

The first decision during the extraction window is what to take. You cannot take everything, and you cannot take it all at once. Prioritization determines which items you extract first and which you leave behind. The standard priority order, from highest to lowest, is:

1. **Mythical pets**: The rarest and most valuable pets, worth more than any other item in the game. Always extract mythical pets first.


2. **Legendary pets**: The second-highest tier, still extremely valuable. Extract legendary pets after mythical.


3. **High-rarity rare pets**: Rare pets with strong income rates or trading value. Extract these next.


4. **Resources and gear**: Cash, materials, and gear that can be used immediately. Extract these if there is still time.


5. **Mid-rarity pets**: Rare and epic pets that are valuable but replaceable. Extract these only if you have time.


6. **Common pets**: The lowest priority, often left behind unless the raid is going extremely well.

The priority order is not absolute. A mythical. The priority order is a default, and you should adapt to the situation.

## The Pet Pickup Mechanic

Pets in Build a Base and Steal are picked up by interacting with them. The interaction takes a short time, during which you are stationary and vulnerable. The pickup is the most dangerous moment of the extraction window, because the defender knows exactly where you are.

### Single-Pet vs. Multi-Pet Pickup

You can carry multiple pets at once, but the carrying capacity depends on your gear. A basic raider can carry one pet at a time. A mid-tier raider can carry two or three. A late-game raider with high-capacity gear can carry five or more. The more pets you can carry, the fewer trips you need to make, which reduces your exposure.

Multi-pet pickup is faster overall, but it has a hidden cost. If you are neutralized during the extraction, you lose all the pets you are carrying. A single-pet pickup is slower, but it limits the loss to one pet per neutralization. Experienced raiders balance the trade-off by carrying a small number of high-value pets and making multiple trips for lower-value pets.

### The Quick Grab Technique

The quick grab is a pickup technique that minimizes the stationary time. Instead of waiting for the full pickup animation, you initiate the pickup and move as soon as the pet is in your inventory time pressure, such as when the defender is approaching or when the raid timer is running low.

The quick grab is also useful in bases with traps that target stationary targets. A stationary raider is a target for spike traps, slow traps, and other stationary-effect defenses. The quick grab keeps you moving, which makes you a harder target.

## Secure Extraction Paths

The path you take to exit the base matters as much as the path you took to enter. Secure extraction paths are pre-planned routes that minimize your exposure during the critical exit period. The standard extraction path is the reverse of your entry path, but this is not always the best choice.

### The Reverse Path

The reverse path is the most reliable extraction route, because you have already navigated it and know the trap locations. The disadvantage is that the defender may have reinforced the path or activated traps behind you. If you entered through the front door, the defender has had time to block or trap the front door during the raid. The reverse path is reliable but predictable.

### The Alternate Path

The alternate path is a different exit route that the defender has not had time to reinforce. If you entered through the front, exit through a side entrance or a breach you made in a non-obvious wall section. The alternate path requires more navigation at the exit.

### The Bypass Path

The bypass path uses lockpicks or electronic bypasses to exit through a locked door that the defender has not reinforced. The bypass path is the stealthiest extraction route, because it requires no breach and generates no noise. The bypass path is most useful when the defender is actively blocking the obvious exits.

## The Defensive Loot

Some loot is not in pet enclosures. Cash, materials, and gear are often stored in chests, vaults, and other containers that require separate interaction. Defensive loot is the term for loot that is guarded by additional defenses, and it requires a different extraction approach.

### Chest Breaking

Chests are storage containers that can be broken to extract the loot inside. The breaking takes time, during which you are stationary. Chest breaking is appropriate when the chest contains high-value items, but it is risky in active defenses. The general rule is to break chests only when you are sure the loot inside is worth the time, which is usually only late-game.

The counter-raid is the defender's response raid against you while you are still extracting. Counter-raids are most common against high-value targets, where the defender is highly motivated to recover their loot. The counter-raid risk is highest in the moments after a successful breach, when the defender has had time to organize a response.

### Detecting the Counter-Raid

The first sign of a counter-raid is the appearance of the defender or their allies at the entrance you used. If you see movement at the entrance, you are likely being counter-raided. The second sign is the sound of breach activity at the entrance, which indicates that the defender is breaching back into the base to recover their loot. The third sign is the appearance of defensive systems, like repair traps or alarm networks, that were not present when you entered. The defender may have activated these in response to your raid.

### Responding to the Counter-Raid

The response to a counter-raid depends on the stage of the extraction. If you are early in the extraction, retreat immediately. You can return later when the counter-raid has passed. If you are late in the extraction, finish what you are carrying and exit through an alternate path. If you are at the vault, grab the highest-value items defender. The defender has home-field advantage, knows the base better than you, and can call on allies. Engaging the defender in a counter-raid is the most common way to turn a profitable raid into a loss.

## Post-Extraction Processing

The extraction is complete when you exit the base, but the raid is not finished. The loot you extracted must be processed: stored securely, evaluated for trading or selling, and added to your collection. Post-extraction processing is the discipline of managing the loot in the minutes and hours after the raid.

### Immediate Storage

The first step is to store the extracted loot in a secure location. Your own base is the obvious choice, but it may be under counter-raid risk. A more secure option is a friend's base, a community storage facility, or a bank system if one is available. Immediate storage prevents the defender from recovering the loot through a counter-raid.

### Loot Evaluation

The second step is to evaluate the loot. Each extracted pet has a market value, a use value, and a trade value. The market value is what you can sell the pet for in the trading system. The use value is what the pet is worth in your own collection, based on its income rate and rarity. The trade value is what other players might offer in a direct trade. Evaluate each pet on all three dimensions to determine the its use value exceeds its market value, which is the case for pets that fill gaps in your collection or that have higher income rates than what you could buy on the market. Sell the pet if its market value exceeds its use value, which is the case for duplicate or low-impact pets. Trade the pet if a direct trade with another player produces a better outcome for both parties.

## Common Extraction Mistakes

The most common extraction mistakes are predictable, and avoiding them is the easiest way to improve your raid profitability.

### The Greed Extraction

The greed extraction is when you take too long trying to extract everything. The defender responds, the raid timer expires, or you are neutralized while carrying high-value pets. The greed extraction is the single most common cause of failed raids. Set a time limit for the extraction, and stick to it. The most profitable raids are the ones where you extract the top-priority loot quickly and exit, not the ones where you extract everything slowly.

### The Static Extraction

The static extraction is when you stay in one spot while picking up loot, giving the defender time to approach and engage. Move during the extraction. Pick up a pet, move to a different section, pick up another pet, and so on. A moving extractor is much harder to counter-raid than a static one.

### The Disorganized Extraction

The disorganized extraction is when you pick up pets in random order, loot. Pick up the highest-value pets first, regardless of order, and exit as soon as you have what you came for.

## Conclusion: The Raid Is Not Over Until the Loot Is Stored

A successful breach is not a successful raid. A successful raid is a successful extraction. The extraction window is the most pressured period of the raid, and the choices you make during that window determine whether you walk away with a profit or a loss. Prioritize the highest-value loot, move quickly through a pre-planned path, and store the loot immediately after exit. The breaching skills matter, but the extraction skills are what turn a raid into a result.
