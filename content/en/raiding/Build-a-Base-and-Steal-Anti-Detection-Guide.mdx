## The Detection Layer in Build a Base and Steal

The detection layer is the network of sensors and alarms that monitor a base for intruder activity. Most players think of traps as the defensive system, but in serious bases, the detection layer is the first line of defense. A raider who triggers the detection layer early in the raid loses the stealth advantage and must complete the raid under pressure.

The detection layer has four main components: alarm traps, surveillance cameras, pressure plates, and audio sensors. Each component detects a different kind of activity, and each requires a different anti-detection technique. Understanding the components is the first step toward defeating them.

## Alarm Traps

Alarm traps are the most common detection component. An alarm trap triggers when an intruder crosses its detection area, producing a sound and notifying the defender. The alarm trap itself does no. The radius is usually a circle of one to three tiles, depending on the trap tier. Motion sensors detect any movement, including walking, running, and crouching. The only movement they do not detect is standing still, which is why experienced stealth raiders pause periodically during their approach.

### Pressure Plate Alarms

Pressure plate alarms trigger when a specific tile is stepped on. The pressure plate is hidden in the floor, and the raider cannot see it. Pressure plates are particularly dangerous because they trigger with even the lightest step, and a careful raider walking on tiptoes will still trigger them. The only way to bypass a pressure plate is to step over it, jump over it, or disable it with an electronic bypass.

### Audio Sensor Alarms

Audio sensor alarms trigger when a specific sound level is reached within a radius. Cutting a wall with a plasma cutter, breaking a lock with a sledgehammer, or even walking with heavy gear all generate enough sound to trigger an audio sensor. The audio sensor is the most restrictive of the alarm types, because it triggers on the very actions that raiders need to take to breach the base.

### Magnetic Sensor Alarms

Magnetic sensor alarms trigger when metallic gear is brought into proximity. Carrying metal gear near a magnetic sensor triggers the way to bypass a magnetic sensor is to use non-metallic gear, but this restricts your tool selection.

## Surveillance Cameras

Surveillance cameras provide visual monitoring of base sections. A camera does not trigger an alarm directly, but it transmits visual information to the defender, who can then respond. Cameras are particularly dangerous because they work even when the raider is stationary, and a camera that has seen the raider is essentially a permanent alarm.

### Camera Types

There are three main camera types in Build a Base and Steal. Fixed cameras monitor a single direction and cannot move. Fixed cameras are the easiest to bypass, because the raider can simply stay out of the camera's field of view. Rotating cameras sweep across a section, and the raider must time movement to occur during the camera's blind spot. Pan-tilt cameras can be controlled by the defender in real time, and they are the most difficult to bypass, because the defender can follow the raider's movement with the camera.

### Camera Detection

A camera is detected by looking for a small lens or a faint glow in the corner of a section. Many cameras are also detectable by the electronic bypass tool, which can identify the camera's signal. seconds at the entrance of each section to identify all visible cameras, and plan the approach accordingly.

### Camera Disabling

Cameras can be disabled in three ways. The first is physical destruction: a sledgehammer or a stone can break a camera. Physical destruction is fast but loud, which alerts other defenses. The second is electronic bypass: the bypass tool can disable the camera's signal, making it appear offline. Electronic bypass is silent and is the preferred method for stealth raiders. The third is jamming: a jamming device produces a signal that overwhelms the camera's transmission, preventing the defender from seeing the feed. Jamming is a temporary solution, because the camera resumes working once the jammer is removed.

## The Detection Network

In serious bases, the detection components are connected into a network. The network amplifies the effectiveness of each component, because triggering one component can activate others. A pressure plate in a corridor may activate cameras in adjacent rooms, and a motion sensor in the entrance may trigger alarm traps deeper in the base. The network is the reason that single-component anti-detection is insufficient; you must defeat the entire network, not just the components you can see.

### The Cascading Alarm

The cascading alarm is the most common network pattern. Triggering one alarm causes nearby can respond. Anti-detection against a cascading alarm requires moving away from the trigger point before the cascade reaches your location, which is often impossible in tight corridors.

### The Zone Alarm

The zone alarm divides the base into sections, and each section has its own alarm system. Triggering an alarm in one section does not affect the other sections, but it does notify the defender that the raider is in the specific section. The zone alarm is less dangerous than the cascading alarm, because the raider can move to a different section after triggering. The zone alarm is most effective when combined with traps, because the zone alarm tells the defender where to focus their attention.

### The Network Jammer

A network jammer is a defensive tool that disrupts the entire detection network within a radius. The jammer is a single-use item that the defender activates, and it disables all detection components in the area for a short period. The jammer is the defender's last-resort response to a stealth raider, and it is the most dangerous tool for the raider, because it forces the raider to either retreat or proceed without any detection feedback.

## Anti-Detection Tools

The raider's anti-detection toolkit includes the electronic bypass, the cloaking device, the noise suppressor, and the sensor scanner. Each tool counters a different detection component, and the most effective anti-detection raiders carry all of them.

### Electronic Bypass

The electronic bypass is the most versatile anti-detection tool. It can disable alarm silent, which is the primary advantage over physical destruction. The bypass also works through walls, which means you can disable a camera in an adjacent room without entering the room.

### Cloaking Device

The cloaking device makes the raider invisible to optical surveillance for a short period. The cloaking does not disable the cameras, but it makes the cameras unable to see the raider. The cloaking device is useful for bypassing pan-tilt cameras, which the raider cannot disable without alerting the defender. The cloaking has a limited duration, and the raider must complete the critical action before the cloaking expires.

### Noise Suppressor

The noise suppressor reduces the sound generated by the raider's actions. The suppressor attaches to gear and reduces the noise of breaching, walking, and other actions. The suppressor does not eliminate sound entirely, but it reduces it to a level that does not trigger audio sensors. The suppressor is essential for raiding bases with extensive audio sensor networks.

### Sensor Scanner

The sensor scanner is a passive tool that detects nearby sensors without triggering them. The scanner produces a faint beep when it is near a sensor, and the closer the scanner is to the sensor, the louder the beep. The scanner is the primary tool for identifying hidden sensors, and it is the reason that a serious stealth raider never enters a base without first scanning the area.

## Anti-Detection Techniques

The techniques below describe how the tools that handles the full detection network.

### The Sensor Sweep

The sensor sweep is the first action of any anti-detection raid. Walk the perimeter of the base slowly with the sensor scanner active, and identify every sensor within detection range. Note the location of each sensor, the type of sensor, and any nearby cameras. The sensor sweep takes a few minutes, but it produces a complete map of the detection layer that you can reference throughout the raid.

### The Cascading Bypass

The cascading bypass is the technique for defeating a cascading alarm network. Start by identifying the most isolated sensor, the one that is least connected to the network. Disable that sensor first, then work toward the more connected sensors. By disabling the isolated sensors first, you reduce the network's reach before you tackle the central nodes. The cascading bypass requires patience, but it is the only reliable way to defeat a dense network.

### The Bypass-and-Breach

The bypass-and-breach technique uses the electronic bypass to disable the sensors in a section, then uses a breaching tool to enter the section. The bypass is silent, the breach is loud, but the sensors in the section are disabled, so the breach does not trigger an alarm. The bypass-and-breach is the standard technique for entering a section with heavy surveillance.

### The Cloak-and-Move

The cloak-and-move technique uses the cloaking device to pass through a camera's field of view without being seen. The cloak is short the raider needs to enter quickly.

## Counter-Defenses to Anti-Detection

Defenders who anticipate stealth raiders use counter-defenses, which are designed to defeat anti-detection techniques. The most common counter-defenses are decoy sensors, redundant sensors, and sensor jammers.

### Decoy Sensors

Decoy sensors are visible sensors placed in obvious locations, designed to attract the raider's attention. The raider spends time disabling the decoy, while the real sensor monitors the section. Decoy sensors are common in mid-game bases, and they catch many inexperienced stealth raiders. The way to identify a decoy is to scan the area with the sensor scanner: a decoy sensor produces no beep, because it is not actually a sensor.

### Redundant Sensors

Redundant sensors are multiple sensors monitoring the same area, so that disabling one does not disable the detection. Redundant sensors are common in late-game bases, and they require the raider to disable every sensor in the section. The technique for defeating redundant sensors is to identify all of them first, then disable them in a sequence that prevents any from triggering.

### Sensor Jammers

Sensor jammers are defensive tools that disrupt the raider's bypass tool. The jammer produces a signal that prevents the bypass from working, and the raider must wait for the jammer to expire or move to a different area. Sensor jammers are rare and expensive, and they are typically used only in that fits the target. The standard sequence is:

1. **Pre-raid intelligence**: Walk the perimeter, identify the base, and evaluate the detection layer.


2. **Sensor sweep**: Scan the area and map every sensor in the raid path.


3. **Bypass planning**: Identify the bypass sequence, including which sensors to disable first and which tools to use.


4. **Entry**: Enter the base through the least-monitored path, using cloak-and-move for any cameras in the way.


5. **Disable sensors in path**: As you move through the base, disable the sensors in your path before they trigger.


6. **Breach and extract**: Once the sensors in your path are disabled, breach the target and extract the loot.


7. **Exit**: Exit through a pre-planned path, disabling any sensors you encounter.

The sequence is not rigid. Experienced anti-detection raiders adapt the sequence to the base, the defenders, and the available tools. The discipline of the sequence is what produces consistent results.

## Common Anti-Detection Mistakes

The most common anti-detection mistakes are predictable, and avoiding them is the difference between a successful stealth raid and a failed one.

### The Over-Reliance on Cloak

The over-reliance on cloak is when the raider uses the cloaking device for everything, ignoring the bypass tool and the noise suppressor. The cloak is a finite suppressor for noise, and the cloak only for cameras that cannot be bypassed.

### The Premature Bypass

The premature bypass is when the raider disables a sensor too early, before the raider is in a position to benefit from the disable. A sensor that is disabled while the raider is still in the outer section gives the defender time to notice the disable and respond. The bypass should happen as close to the raider's arrival as possible, to minimize the defender's response window.

### The Ignored Sensor

The ignored sensor is when the raider assumes a section has no sensors and walks through it without scanning. The first sensor the raider encounters is often a hidden pressure plate or a magnetic sensor that was not visible from outside. Always scan every section before walking through it, even if the section appears clear.

## Conclusion: The Invisible Raid Is the Profitable Raid

Anti-detection is the most demanding discipline in Build a Base and Steal raiding, but it is also the most rewarding. A raider who can defeat the detection layer can raid the most valuable bases in the server without alerting the defender, and the resulting extractions are consistently profitable. The detection layer is the defender's most powerful tool, and the anti-detection techniques are the raider's most powerful counter. Master the techniques, build the right toolkit, and the most heavily monitored bases become the most profitable targets.
