Every Build a Base and Steal raider eventually faces a fundamental choice: stealth or brute force. Stealth raiding is the careful, patient, low-noise approach that bypass valid playstyles, and the best raiders learn to use both depending on the situation. This guide explains how each style works, when to use it, and how to switch between them mid-raid when the situation changes.

## The Stealth Philosophy

Stealth raiding is built on the principle that the defender is more dangerous than the walls. A wall can be broken with the right tool, but a defender who knows you are coming can repair walls, activate traps, relocate valuables, and even counter-raid while you are stuck inside their base. The stealth approach minimizes the defender's advantage by minimizing their awareness. A defender who does not know you are raiding cannot respond, and a base that is not actively defended is much easier to navigate.

The core disciplines of stealth are patience, observation, and timing. You wait for the right moment to enter, you observe the base's patterns before you commit, and you move at a pace that avoids detection. These disciplines are not natural to most players, because they require suppressing the impulse to act quickly. The most successful stealth raiders are those who have learned to treat the first phase of a raid as a study, not an attack.

### When Stealth Wins

Stealth is the superior approach in several specific situations. repair faster than you can break. The silent approach bypasses the defender entirely, because they do not know to respond. The second is when the target's defenses are uneven. A base with strong walls but weak traps is a poor candidate for brute force, because you will spend all your time on walls. The same base may be a good candidate for stealth, because the trap coverage is poor and the silent approach bypasses the walls entirely.

The third situation is when the loot is highly concentrated. If the valuables are in a single vault at the center of the base, the brute force approach forces you to break through every layer to reach them. The stealth approach can target the vault directly, bypassing the outer layers. The fourth is when your gear is limited. A new player with basic gear cannot afford the gear cost of a brute force raid, but they can afford the time cost of a stealth approach.

### When Stealth Fails

Stealth fails in several situations, and recognizing them is important. Stealth fails when the target has extensive surveillance that detects all silent movement. A base with a dense alarm network cannot be stealth-raided, because every step triggers an alarm. Stealth also fails when the target's defenses require you to move quickly. Some trap designs force slow to outpace the trap. Stealth also fails when the loot is scattered throughout the base rather than concentrated. A silent approach that bypasses the vault is not useful if the valuables are not in the vault.

## The Brute Force Philosophy

Brute force raiding is built on the opposite principle: the wall is the obstacle, and the right tool breaks any wall. The defender's response is a secondary concern, because even a fast response cannot repair a wall faster than a skilled breacher can break it. The brute force approach is fast, loud, and gear-intensive, and it is the most direct path from outside the base to the vault.

The core disciplines of brute force are tool selection, sustained damage, and momentum. You choose the right tool for the wall, you apply sustained damage until the wall falls, and you maintain forward momentum through every layer of the base. These disciplines require gear investment and combat awareness, because you will take damage from traps and you may face active defender resistance.

### When Brute Force Wins

Brute force is the superior approach when the target has weak or moderate defenses. A base with one wall layer and a few traps is faster to brute force than to stealth raid, because the stealth setup time exceeds the brute Brute force is also superior when you have a significant gear advantage, because the gear cost is low relative to the value you are extracting.

Brute force wins in late game when most bases have multi-layer defenses that are designed to slow but not stop a determined raider. A late-game raider with high-tier gear can break through most defenses in minutes, and the time saved compared to stealth is significant.

### When Brute Force Fails

Brute force fails when the defender is highly active and has time to repair. A defender who can repair faster than you can break is a defender who will eventually force you to retreat. Brute force also fails when the base has resource attrition defenses that drain your gear over time. A base with electric traps in every layer can drain your gear charges faster than you can replace them, and you will eventually run out of gear before you reach the vault. Brute force also fails when the target's value is too low to justify the gear cost. A base with only common pets is not worth spending 5,000 cash in gear to breach.

## The Hybrid Approach

The most successful raiders in Build a Base and Steal are not pure stealth or pure brute force. They are hybrid raiders who can switch between approaches mid-raid based on the situation. A hybrid raid might begin with a stealth approach to bypass the outer defenses, switch to brute force to break a particularly requires proficiency in both styles and the judgment to know when to switch. But the hybrid approach is also the most flexible, and the most profitable in the long run. A hybrid raider can adapt to any target, while a pure raider is constrained to targets that fit their style.

### Reading the Raid in Real Time

The key to the hybrid approach is reading the raid in real time and deciding when to switch. The signals that indicate a switch are:

- **Detection events**: If you trigger an alarm, the stealth approach has failed, and you should switch to brute force to finish quickly.


- **Time pressure**: If you are taking too long on a stealth approach, switch to brute force to make up time.


- **Damage taken**: If you have taken significant damage, switch to a faster approach to minimize further damage.


- **Gear depletion**: If your stealth tools are running low, switch to brute force tools that may be more available.

The signals that indicate staying with the current approach are:

- **Successful progression**: If you are moving through the base without triggering alarms, continue with stealth.


- **Defender inactivity**: If the defender has not responded, your current approach is working, and you should not switch.

A brute force-only loadout has no detection tools, and you cannot gather the intelligence needed for stealth. The best loadouts are balanced, with tools for both approaches.

### Stealth Loadout

A stealth loadout includes: lockpick set, electronic bypass, plasma cutter (for occasional wall cutting), cloaking device (for brief invisibility), noise suppressors, and detection gear (for intelligence gathering). A stealth loadout is light, because stealth requires you to move quickly and quietly. Heavy gear slows you down and generates noise.

### Brute Force Loadout

A brute force loadout includes: sledgehammer, plasma cutter, breaching charges, armor (for trap damage), healing supplies, and gear replenishment (for sustained operations). A brute force loadout is heavy, because brute force requires you to take hits and keep moving. Light gear is insufficient for sustained combat.

A hybrid loadout includes the essentials of both: a basic breaching tool (plasma cutter or sledgehammer), a basic stealth tool (lockpick or electronic bypass), light armor, healing supplies, and a small number of breaching charges for emergencies. The choice between stealth, brute force, and hybrid should be made before you commit to the raid, based on your evaluation of the target. The signals that indicate each style are:

- **Stealth signals**: Inactive defender, weak outer walls, strong inner walls, concentrated loot, limited gear, late-night server time.

## Switching Mid-Raid

Switching approaches mid-raid is the most advanced technique, and it requires practice to execute smoothly. The switch happens at a natural transition point, typically when you reach a new section of the base or when the current approach has reached its limit.

### The Stealth-to-Brute Switch

The stealth-to-brute switch happens when stealth has explosive, or a sustained attack. The defender is now aware of you, and the rest of the raid is a brute force race. The switch is most effective when you are close to the vault, because the brute force approach can power through the final walls quickly.

### The Brute-to-Stealth Switch

The brute-to-stealth switch happens when brute force is no longer working, typically because you have run low on gear or because the defender is responding too quickly. The switch is initiated by retreating to a hidden position, waiting for the defender to disengage, and then resuming the approach with stealth tools. The switch requires patience, because you must wait for the defender to lose interest. The switch is most effective when you have a clear exit path, because the goal of the switch is to extract with whatever loot you have rather than to push deeper.

## Conclusion: Style Is Strategy, Not Preference

The choice between stealth and brute force in Build a Base and Steal is a strategic decision, not a personal preference. The best raiders learn both approaches, evaluate the target to choose the right approach, and switch between approaches when the situation changes. A pure stealth raider cannot raid heavily defended bases. A pure brute force raider cannot raid active defenders. A hybrid raider can raid anything, and that flexibility is what makes them the most successful.
