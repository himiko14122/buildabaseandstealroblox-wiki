## Understanding Breaching Mechanics

Breaching in Build a Base and Steal is the act of removing or bypassing a wall section to gain entry to the base. Walls can be broken with damage-dealing tools, bypassed with lockpicks or cutters, or destroyed outright with explosives. Each approach has a different cost, time, and risk profile, and the choice of approach depends on the wall material, the surrounding defenses, and the gear you have available.

The fundamental breaching trade-off is between speed and noise. Fast breaching methods, like explosives, generate a lot of noise and alert the defender. Slow breaching methods, like cutting, generate less noise but take more time and leave you exposed. There is no universally best breaching method; the best method is the one that fits the specific situation.

## Wall-Breaking Tools

The most common breaching tools are the wall-breaking gear items, which deal damage to wall sections. Each tool has a damage value, a noise level, and a cooldown. The damage value determines how much of the wall's hit points the tool removes per use. The noise level determines how loud the breaching action is, which affects whether nearby defenders hear moderate noise, and has a short cooldown. The sledgehammer is the workhorse breaching tool, and most raiders carry at least one. It is appropriate for wood and stone walls, where its damage is sufficient to break through in a reasonable time. Against metal or reinforced alloy walls, the sledgehammer is too slow to be practical.

The sledgehammer's main advantage is reliability. It is cheap, easy to use, and has no special requirements. The main disadvantage is that it is loud. A sledgehammer breach can be heard across most of the map, which alerts any active defender. Use the sledgehammer when speed matters more than stealth, and when the target is unlikely to have an active defender.

### The Plasma Cutter

The plasma cutter is a mid-tier wall-breaking tool. It deals high damage, generates low noise, and has a medium cooldown. The plasma cutter is the go-to tool for breaking metal walls, and it is also effective against reinforced alloy when you do not have explosives available. The plasma cutter's main advantage is its damage-to-noise ratio: it breaks walls quickly while generating relatively little sound. The main disadvantage is that it requires power, which means you must carry power cells or recharge periodically.

The plasma cutter is the preferred tool for late-game raiders, because most late gear to reduce the noise even further, and you can breach walls without alerting most defenders.

### The Breaching Charge

The breaching charge is an explosive tool. It deals very high damage to a small area, generates very high noise, and has a long cooldown because each charge is single-use. The breaching charge is the fastest way to break a wall section, and it is appropriate when you need to create a large breach quickly. The breaching charge is also the loudest tool, which means it alerts every defender in the area and often triggers automatic defense systems.

The breaching charge is most effective when used in combination with other tools. A common sequence is to use a breaching charge to create a wide opening, then use a plasma cutter to expand the opening or break adjacent walls. The combination of explosive and cutting is significantly faster than either alone.

### The Hydraulic Ram

The hydraulic ram is a specialized tool that deals high damage to a single point and generates moderate noise. The ram is mounted on a wall and then activated, dealing a single large damage instance. The ram is slower than a plasma cutter but cheaper to maintain, and it is appropriate for sustained breaching operations where you are working through a long wall section.

### The Lockpick Set

The lockpick set is the basic bypass tool. It works on standard locks, takes a moderate amount of time, and generates almost no noise. The lockpick set is the preferred entry method for stealth raiders, because it does not alert defenders. The downside is that it does not work on advanced locks, and it can be detected by surveillance systems that monitor lock access.

The lockpick set is most effective when the target uses standard locks on multiple entry points. Pick the most isolated entry point first, because the lockpick sound is the only noise you generate, and an isolated entry point is less likely to be monitored.

### The Electronic Bypass

### Breaching Charge Placement

The placement of a breaching charge is the most important factor in its effectiveness. A charge placed at the weakest point of a wall deals more damage than a charge placed at the strongest point. The weakest point is usually a corner, a section near a wall joint, or a section that has been previously damaged. Look for these weak points when placing charges, and you will get more value out of every charge.

Breaching charges also have a blast radius, which means they can damage adjacent walls, traps, and pets. Be careful when placing charges near the vault or near high-value pet enclosures, because the blast can destroy the loot you are trying to steal. The best explosive specialists learn to place charges that maximize wall damage while minimizing loot damage.

When all three charges detonate simultaneously, they create a much larger breach than three separate detonations.

## Breaching Sequences

The breaching sequence is the order in which you breach different parts of a base. The sequence is more important than the individual breaches, because a well-ordered sequence compounds your advantage while a poorly-ordered sequence wastes time and gear.

### The Classic Sequence: Outer Wall, Inner Wall, Vault

The classic sequence breaks the outer wall first, then the inner wall, then the vault. This sequence is straightforward but expensive, because it requires breaching three separate defenses. The advantage is that it is reliable, and a skilled raider can execute the classic sequence even against heavily defended bases. The disadvantage is the time and gear cost, which can be high.

The classic sequence is most effective when the walls between the sections are of different materials. If the outer wall is wood and the inner wall is stone, you can break the outer wall quickly and then take your time on the inner wall. If both walls are reinforced alloy, the classic sequence is too expensive to be practical.

### The Skim Sequence: Outer Wall, Nearest Pet

The skim sequence breaks the outer wall and then grabs the nearest pet without trying to reach the vault with valuable pets in the outer section, which is a common configuration for newer players.

The skim sequence has a hidden benefit: it leaves the vault intact, which means the target is still profitable in future sessions. A defender who is skimmed is less likely to be upset than a defender who is fully raided, and the relationship can remain positive.

### The Surgical Sequence: Bypass to Vault, Breach to Exit

The surgical sequence bypasses the outer defenses using lockpicks or electronic bypass, reaches the vault through hidden paths or weak points, breaches only the vault, and exits with the valuables. The surgical sequence is the most efficient in terms of time and gear, but it requires the most skill and the most reconnaissance. The surgical sequence is the domain of experienced raiders, and it is the approach that produces the highest profit-per-minute.

The surgical sequence depends on accurate intelligence. You must know where the vault is, what defenses surround it, and which entry points are accessible. Without that intelligence, the surgical sequence fails, and you are left exposed in the middle of a base with no clear path out.

## Defensive Awareness During Breaching

Breaching is the moment when you### The Sound Check

Before you start a breach, perform a sound check. Move a few steps away from the intended breach point and listen for any nearby activity. If you hear movement, sounds, or other indicators of an active defender, wait or choose a different breach point. If the area is quiet, you have a few minutes to breach before the defender is likely to respond.

### The Cover Check

Before you start a breach, identify a cover position. The cover is a spot you can move to quickly if the defender responds. Walls, corners, and pet enclosures are all potential cover. Choose the cover before you start, and rehearse the movement to it. A raider who has identified cover in advance responds to defender action in seconds; a raider who has not identified cover is exposed for tens of seconds while they figure out where to go.

### The Tool Switch

Have multiple tools available and switch between them based on the situation. The sledgehammer for wood and stone, the plasma cutter for metal, the breaching charge for emergencies, the lockpick for locked doors. A raider who carries the right tool for each situation is faster and more efficient than a raider who relies on a single tool.

## Common Breaching Mistakes

The most common breaching mistakes are predictable breaching charge on a base that is best approached quietly. The defender is alerted, the response is fast, and the raid becomes a race against time that the raider usually loses. Match the noise level of your breach to the activity level of the defender. Inactive defenders tolerate any breach; active defenders tolerate only silent breaches.

### The Under-Prepared Breach

The under-prepared breach attempts to break a wall without the appropriate tool. A raider using a sledgehammer on reinforced alloy is wasting time and gear. A raider using a lockpick on a reinforced alloy door is failing to enter at all. Check the wall material and the lock type before you commit to a breach, and ensure you have the right tool.

### The Static Breach

The static breach is when you stand in one spot and use the same tool for an extended period. The defender knows exactly where you are, has time to respond, and can focus all defenses on that spot. Move during the breach: switch tools, change position, and use different angles. A dynamic breacher is much harder to defend against than a static one.

## Conclusion: Breaching Is Sequence, Not Just Force

Successful breaching in Build a Base and Steal is not about having the strongest tool. It is about choosing base quickly with the right combination, and an unskilled raider will fail against a lightly defended base with the wrong combination. Learn the tools, learn the sequences, and learn to read the defensive situation. The breach that succeeds is rarely the breach with the most force; it is the breach that fits the moment.
