Counter-raiding is the practice of attacking a raider while they are in the middle of raiding your base, and it is one of the most dangerous situations in Build a Base and Steal. A counter-raid can catch the raider off guard, force them to use their healing items, and potentially stop the raid entirely. But counter-raiding also has risks, because the defender must leave their base to engage the raider, and the engagement is in a confined space where the raider has the home advantage. This guide covers counter-raid detection, the response options, the emergency defense tactics, and the long-term strategy for protecting against counter-raids.

## How Counter-Raids Work

A counter-raid is initiated when the defender of a base detects that an active raid is in progress and decides to engage the raider. The defender is usually notified by the alarm trap or by the in-game notification system, and the defender has a few minutes to respond before the raid is complete.

The counter-raid is launched through a special interface that is only available when a raid is in progress on your base. The interface shows the location of the raider within your base, the time remaining in the raid, and the gear you have available for the counter-raid. The counter-raid consumes your own raid energy and gear, which means it has a real cost.

The counter-raid spawns the defender at the entrance to their own base, and the defender must navigate through the base to reach the raider. The defender can use their own traps and walls against the raider, which gives the defender a home advantage. The raider must also navigate the traps while watching for the defender, which splits their attention.

### Detecting an Active Raid

The first step in counter-raiding is detecting the active raid. Without detection, the counter-raid cannot be launched, and the defender has no opportunity to respond. The detection mechanisms in Build a Base and Steal are the alarm trap, the in-game notification, and the visual observation.

The alarm trap is the most reliable detection mechanism, because it triggers automatically when the raider enters the trap zone. The alarm trap should be placed near the entrance of the base, so it triggers as soon as the raid begins. The alarm notification appears in the defender's interface, and it provides the time and location of the trigger.

The in-game notification is the second most reliable mechanism, and it appears automatically when a raid begins on your base. The notification is sent to the defender regardless of whether they have an alarm trap, and it includes a basic prompt to launch a counter-raid. The notification is the easiest detection mechanism, because it requires no setup.

The visual observation is the least reliable but the most informative. The defender who happens to be near their base when a raid starts can see the raider enter and can respond based on the direct observation. The visual observation is rare, because most defenders are not near their base when a raid starts, but it is the most informative form of detection.

### Responding to a Raid

Once a raid is detected, the defender has several response options. The response should be chosen based on the situation, and the choice has real consequences for the outcome of the raid.

The first response option is to launch a counter-raid. The counter-raid engages the raider directly and forces them to either fight or retreat. The counter-raid is the most aggressive response, and it has the highest potential payoff (stopping the raid entirely) but also the highest cost (consuming your own gear and energy).

The second response option is to reinforce the base remotely. Some defensive items can be placed or activated remotely, which strengthens the defense while the raid is in progress. The remote reinforcement is less aggressive than the counter-raid, but it is also less costly, and it can be enough to stop a less determined raider.

The third response option is to log out and accept the loss. Logging out ends your opportunity to respond, but it also protects your own gear and energy from being consumed. The log-out response is appropriate when the raid is too far advanced to stop, or when the loot in the base is not worth the cost of the counter-raid.

The fourth response option is to wait and observe. Some defenders prefer to watch the raid in progress, gathering information about the raider's tactics and gear, and using the information to improve their base design for the future. The observation response is the least costly, but it does not stop the current raid.

### Emergency Defense Tactics

If the defender decides to launch a counter-raid, the emergency defense tactics determine the outcome. The most important tactic is positioning, which means placing yourself in a location that gives you the advantage. The best positions are near chokepoints, where the raider must pass through a narrow space, and near trap clusters, where the raider will take damage while engaging you.

The second most important tactic is timing, which means launching the counter-raid at the right moment in the raid. The best timing is when the raider is in the middle of breaching a wall, because they are stationary and distracted. The worst timing is when the raider is near the entrance, because they can retreat quickly.

The third most important tactic is gear selection, which means choosing the right gear for the counter-raid. The optimal gear for a counter-raid is similar to the optimal gear for a regular raid, with a focus on burst damage and survivability. The counter-raid is usually short, so high-damage weapons and shields are more valuable than sustained-damage weapons.

### The Trap Synergy

The trap synergy is the combination of your traps and your counter-raid presence. The traps force the raider to take damage or use healing items, and the counter-raid forces them to engage you while they are already weakened. The combined effect is much stronger than either alone.

The trap synergy is most effective when the raider has already triggered several traps before the counter-raid begins. The damaged raider is easier to defeat in direct combat, and the counter-raid can finish them off quickly. The synergy requires that the raider is committed to the raid (not near the entrance), which means the counter-raid should be launched when the raider is in the middle of the base.

The trap synergy is least effective when the raider has not triggered any traps. The undamaged raider can engage the defender on equal terms, and the counter-raid may not be enough to stop them. The defender who wants to maximize the trap synergy should design the base so that any raider who reaches the middle of the base has triggered several traps.

## The Defensive Cooldown

Counter-raids have a cooldown period, which is the time you must wait before you can launch another counter-raid. The cooldown is short (usually 30 seconds to a few minutes), but it prevents the defender from spamming counter-raids against a determined attacker. The cooldown also gives the raider time to extract loot and exit before the next counter-raid can be launched.

The defensive cooldown means that the defender has a limited number of counter-raid opportunities during a single enemy raid. The first counter-raid is the most likely to succeed, because the raider is caught off guard. The second counter-raid is less likely to succeed, because the raider is now prepared. The third and subsequent counter-raids are usually not worth launching, because the raider has adapted to the threat.

The cooldown strategy is to make the first counter-raid count. The defender should commit their best gear and their best tactics to the first counter-raid, and accept that subsequent counter-raids are less effective. The single high-quality counter-raid is usually more effective than multiple low-quality counter-raids.

## Long-Term Anti-Counter Defense

The long-term anti-counter defense is built into the base design. The design features that make the base hard to raid also make the base hard to counter-raid, because the raider must spend so much time on the breach that they are vulnerable to a counter-raid by the time the defender responds.

The long-term defense also includes the use of the alarm trap network, which provides early warning of incoming raids. The alarm network should be placed throughout the base, with alarms at the entrance, in the middle buffer, and near the inner vault. The multiple alarms give the defender multiple opportunities to respond, and the timing of the alarms tells the defender where the raider is in the breach path.

The long-term defense also includes the reputation factor. A defender who is known to launch aggressive counter-raids will be raided less often, because the raider community shares information about counter-raiders. The reputation is a passive deterrent, and it can be as effective as any trap or wall.

## The Counter-Raid Economics

The counter-raid economics is the analysis of whether a counter-raid is worth the cost. The analysis has three components: the cost of the counter-raid, the value of the protected loot, and the probability of stopping the raid.

The cost of the counter-raid is the gear and energy consumed, which is similar to the cost of a regular raid. The value of the protected loot is the cash value of the pets and resources in the base, which is the value the defender is trying to protect. The probability of stopping the raid is the likelihood that the counter-raid will force the raider to retreat, which depends on the defender's skill, the raider's preparation, and the base's defensive features.

A counter-raid is worth launching when the value of the protected loot exceeds the cost of the counter-raid multiplied by the probability of stopping the raid. If the protected loot is worth 20,000 cash and the counter-raid costs 5,000 cash with a 50 percent probability of success, the expected value of the counter-raid is 10,000 cash (50 percent of 20,000), which exceeds the 5,000 cash cost. The counter-raid is worth launching.

If the protected loot is worth 2,000 cash and the counter-raid costs 5,000 cash with a 50 percent probability of success, the expected value of the counter-raid is 1,000 cash, which is less than the 5,000 cash cost. The counter-raid is not worth launching, and the defender should accept the loss.
