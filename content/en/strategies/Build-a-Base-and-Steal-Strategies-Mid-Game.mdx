The mid game in Build a Base and Steal is the period between the first week and the end-game progression, usually months 1 to 3 of play. This is when your account transitions from a basic foundation to a serious contender, and the strategies you adopt during this period determine whether you will be ready for the end game or stuck in mid-tier limbo. This guide covers the priorities, milestones, and common mistakes of the mid game.

## The Mid Game Mindset

The mid game mindset is fundamentally different from the early game mindset. During the early game, the focus is on learning the basics and building a foundation. During the mid game, the focus is on optimization and scaling. The decisions you make during the mid game are about efficiency, not about survival.

The mid game player has three priorities: maximizing income, minimizing losses, and preparing for the end game. Maximizing income means choosing pets, base designs, and trading strategies that produce the most cash per unit of time. Minimizing losses means avoiding raids, scams, and bad trades that drain your resources. Preparing for the end game means building the reserves, the relationships, and the skills that you will need to compete with the top players, because they need to focus their time and resources on the systems that they are best at. A mid game player who tries to be a generalist usually ends up spread too thin, while a mid game player who specializes usually ends up with a strong account in their chosen area.

### Pet Collection Strategy

The mid game pet collection strategy should focus on completing one epic family and at least two rare families. The epic family is the foundation of your mid game income, and the rare families provide diversification and trading flexibility.

The epic family should be one with a strong collection bonus and a manageable completion cost. The current top epic families are the Cosmic Cats family and the Crystal Creatures family, both of which provide meaningful income boosts and have reasonable drop rates. Completing one of these families is a multi-month project that requires consistent rolling, but the income boost is worth the investment.

The rare families should be chosen for their collection bonuses and their trading value. The Dragon Den family and the Phoenix Flock family are typical mid game rare family choices, because they provide meaningful collection bonuses and their pets are actively traded in the market. Completing two rare families is a more achievable goal than completing one epic family, and it provides a strong foundation for the mid game economy.

The pet collection strategy should also include a trading component. Mid can fund a significant portion of your rolling budget, and it is the foundation of the trader playstyle.

### Base Building Strategy

The mid game base building strategy should focus on creating a defensive structure that can resist most casual raids and deter most skilled raids. The mid game base should have at least 3 layers of walls, multiple trap types, and a vault system for protecting the most valuable pets.

The wall layers should be designed to slow down raiders, not to stop them completely. The goal of a mid game base is not to be impenetrable; the goal is to make raiding more expensive than the loot is worth. A raider who has to spend 3 minutes and 5 breaching charges to reach your vault will usually give up and find an easier target.

The trap types should be varied to force raiders to use multiple tools. A base with only one trap type is easy to counter, because the raider can use a single defense disabler to neutralize all the traps. A base with multiple trap types forces the raider to use multiple defense disablers, which is expensive and time-consuming.

The vault system should be designed to protect your most valuable pets, not to store your entire collection. A vault that protects 2 to 3 of your most valuable pets is much more secure than a vault that tries to protect 10 pets, because the raider has to focus their efforts on a single target. A focused vault defense is much more effective than a spread to 50 percent of your rolling budget, which reduces the pressure on your other income sources.

The trading strategy should be based on market awareness and disciplined execution. Market awareness means staying up to date on the current meta, the current prices, and the current trends. Disciplined execution means buying and selling according to your plan, not according to your feelings.

The most common mid game trading strategy is the family completion arbitrage, where you buy pets that complete popular family collections and sell them at a markup. This strategy is low risk and provides a steady income, and it is a good foundation for more advanced trading strategies.

A more advanced strategy is the meta flip, where you buy pets that are about to become valuable due to a balance patch or a new collection bonus, and sell them after the price spike. This strategy is higher risk but much higher reward, and it requires a deep understanding of the meta and the ability to predict how the patch will affect pet values.

### Raiding Strategy

The mid game raiding strategy should focus on consistent, low-risk raids that generate a steady income. The mid game player should not be attempting to take down the toughest bases in the game; the focus should be on taking down similar-progression players with weak defenses.

The raid target selection should be based on a specific set of criteria: the base should have weak walls, few active defenders, and a small but valuable pet collection. The raid should be planned based on the expected loot, the expected cost, and the expected clear exit criteria. The objective might be to breach the vault, grab the most valuable pet, and extract. The exit criteria might be to extract after 5 minutes, or after taking 50 percent damage, or after the vault has been emptied. The plan keeps the raid focused and prevents the most common mid game raiding mistake, which is staying in a failing raid for too long.

### Common Mid Game Mistakes

The most common mid game mistake is over-rolling for a specific pet. A mid game player who decides to chase a legendary pet without the cash reserves to do so usually ends up with a depleted reserve and no legendary. The fix is to set a per-pet rolling budget and to walk away if the budget is exhausted without success.

The second most common mid game mistake is neglecting the base in favor of the pet collection. A mid game player who focuses entirely on pets often ends up with a strong collection and a weak base, which is a bad combination because the base is what protects the collection. The fix is to allocate at least 30 percent of your time and cash to base improvements.

The third most common mid game mistake is poor trading decisions. A mid game player who sells valuable pets for short-term cash often ends up with a depleted trading portfolio and a long-term loss. The fix is to research the market before every trade, and to avoid selling pets that have long-term collection value.

## Mid Game Milestones

By the end of month 3, you should have hit several specific milestones. If you are missing any of these milestonesible against most raiders.

Milestone 3: A trading portfolio that generates at least 30 percent of your rolling budget. This milestone indicates that you have the trading skills to support your pet collection.

Milestone 4: A cash reserve of at least 50,000 cash. This milestone indicates that you have the financial cushion to take advantage of opportunities.

Milestone 5: At least 50 successful raids with a positive raid net income. This milestone indicates that you have the raiding skills to support your income.

## Transitioning to the End Game

The mid game ends when you are ready to start the end game, which is usually around month 3 to 6. The transition to the end game requires three things: a complete epic family, a substantial cash reserve, and a strong trading portfolio. A mid game player who has hit all three of these milestones is ready to start chasing legendary pets, and to start building a base that can resist the toughest raiders in the game.

The transition to the end game should be gradual, not sudden. A mid game player who tries to jump straight to end game content usually fails, because the end game requires skills and resources that take time to develop. The player who transitions gradually, completing one end game goal at a time, usually succeeds.
