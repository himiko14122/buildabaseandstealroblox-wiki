The end game player has four priorities: acquiring the rarest pets, building the strongest bases, dominating the trading market, and maintaining a top leaderboard ranking. Each priority serves a specific purpose in the end game economy, and the player who excels at all four is the player who is at the top of the server.

### Base Fortification

The end game base building strategy should focus on creating a defensive structure that can resist the toughest raids in the game. The end game base should have at least 5 layers of walls, multiple trap types, a vault system, and a counter-attack capability.

The wall layers should be designed to slow down raiders to the point where the raid becomes uneconomical. The goal of an end game base is not to be impenetrable; the goal is to make raiding more expensive than the loot is worth. A raider who has to spend 30 minutes and 20 breaching charges to reach your vault will usually give up and find an easier target, even if your walls can than a base with 5 of the same trap type. The end game player should design their base around the trap positions, not the other way around.

The vault system should be designed to protect the most valuable pets, with multiple layers of defense and a counter-attack capability. The counter-attack capability is a special feature of end game bases, where the defender can spawn a pet that automatically attacks any raider who enters the vault. The counter-attack is a powerful deterrent, and it is one of the most effective defensive features in the game.

### Trading Dominance

The end game trading strategy should focus on dominating the trading market and generating substantial income. The end game player should be one of the top traders in the server, with a portfolio that is large enough to influence the market.

The trading strategy should be based on a deep understanding of the meta, the market, and the psychology of the trading community. The end game player should know which pets are about to become valuable, which pets are about to lose value, and which trades are likely to be accepted by the other party.

The most profitable end game trading strategy is the meta flip, where you buy pets that are about to become valuable due to a balance patch or a new collection bonus, and sell them after the price spike. This strategy requires a deep understanding of the meta and the ability to predict how the patch will affect pet values, and the rewards can be enormous.

A more sustainable strategy is the long-term hold, where you buy pets that are currently undervalued but are expected to become valuable in the long term. This strategy requires patience and a willingness to hold pets for months at a time, but the rewards can be enormous if you correctly identify the next big pet.

## Common End Game Mistakes

The most common end game mistake is over-investing in a single aspect of the game. An end game player who focuses entirely on pet collection often ends up with a strong collection and a weak base, which is a bad combination. The fix is to balance your investments across pets, base, trading, and raiding.

The second most common end game mistake is ignoring the meta. The end game player who does not stay up to date on the meta is the end game player who falls behind. The fix is to read the patch notes carefully, follow the trading community, and adjust your strategy as the meta shifts.

The third most common end game mistake is poor risk management. The end game player who takes excessive risks with their cash reserves often ends up losing everything in a single bad trade or a single bad raid. The fix is to maintain a substantial cash reserve and to avoid risking more than 10 percent of your total value on any single decision.
