## The Trader Mindset

The trader mindset is fundamentally different from the raider or builder mindset. Raiders focus on combat and breach efficiency, builders focus on defensive structure and trap placement, but traders focus on information and timing. The trader who has the best information and the best timing is the trader who makes the most profit.

The trader's primary asset is information. The trader who knows which pets are about to become valuable, which pets are about to lose value, and which trades are likely to be accepted by the other party is the trader who can consistently profit from the market. The trader who does not have this information is the trader who consistently loses money.

The trader's second asset is patience. The trader who waits for the right opportunity, instead of taking every trade that comes along, is the trader who maximizes their profit per trade. The trader who is impatient usually ends up taking bad trades, which over time erodes their portfolio.

The trader's third asset is discipline. The trader who sticks to their plan, even when the market is volatile or the trades are tempting, is the trader who survives the long term. The trader who lacks discipline usually ends up making emotional decisions, which over time destroys their portfolio.

### Building a Trading Portfolio

A trading portfolio in Build a Base and Steal is a collection of pets that the trader holds for trading purposes, rather than for collection or income purposes. The portfolio should be diversified across multiple pet tiers the 60-30-10 rule. 60 percent of the portfolio should be in stable, liquid pets that trade frequently and at predictable prices. These are the foundation of the portfolio, and they provide a steady income through small, reliable trades.

30 percent of the portfolio should be in growth pets, which are pets that are expected to increase in value over the medium term. These are the higher-risk, higher-reward portion of the portfolio, and they provide the bulk of the portfolio's growth.

10 percent of the portfolio should be in speculative pets, which are pets that are expected to either skyrocket in value or become worthless. These are the highest-risk, highest-reward portion of the portfolio, and they provide the occasional big win that can transform the portfolio.

### Market Analysis

Market analysis is the foundation of the trader playstyle, and the trader who does it well is the trader who consistently makes profitable trades. Market analysis involves three main activities: tracking the meta, tracking the prices, and tracking the community.

Tracking the meta involves staying up to date on the current state of the game, including the latest balance patches, the latest collection bonuses, and the latest event changes. The meta directly affects pet values, and the trader who knows the meta can predict which pets are about to become valuable.

Tracking the prices involves monitoring the trading hub for the current prices of the pets in your portfolio and the pets you are considering buying. The trader who tracks the prices can identify undervalued pets and overvalued pets, and they can make market information faster than the official sources, and the trader who is part of the community can take advantage of opportunities that other traders miss.

### Trade Identification

Trade identification is the process of finding specific trades that are likely to be profitable. The most common trade identification strategies are the family completion arbitrage, the meta flip, and the long-term hold.

The family completion arbitrage is the simplest and most reliable trade identification strategy. The trader identifies a pet that is part of a popular family collection, and they buy the pet at a discount because the seller does not realize its value. The trader then sells the pet to a player who is trying to complete the family collection, at a markup that reflects the pet's true value.

The meta flip is a more advanced strategy that requires a deep understanding of the meta. The trader identifies a pet that is about to become valuable due to a balance patch or a new collection bonus, and they buy the pet before the price spike. The trader then sells the pet after the price spike, when the market has caught up to the new value.

The long-term hold is the most patient strategy, and it is the strategy that generates the biggest wins. The trader identifies a pet that is currently undervalued but is expected to become valuable in the long term, and they buy the pet and hold it for months or years. The trader then sells the pet when the long-term value has been realized, often at a price that is many times the original purchase price.

### Negotiation Tactics

Negotiation is the art of convincing the other party to accept a trade that is favorable to you. The trader who is a good negotiator can extract better prices, accept more favorable trades, and avoid being taken advantage of. The trader who is a poor negotiator usually ends up with worse prices, which over time erodes their portfolio.

The most reject lowball offers and accept fair offers, which prevents the most common negotiation mistake, which is accepting a bad trade because the trader is not sure what the pet is worth.

The second most important tactic is to be patient. The trader who is willing to walk away from a bad trade is the trader who will eventually find a good trade. The trader who is impatient and accepts the first offer that comes along usually ends up with worse trades than the trader who waits for a better offer.

The third most important tactic is to be polite. The trading community is small, and the trader who has a reputation for being polite and professional is the trader who will be offered the best trades in the future. The trader who is rude or aggressive usually gets passed over for the better trades.

## Common Trader Mistakes

The most common trader mistake is buying a pet without researching its value. A trader who offers 100 rolls for a pet that has been selling for 30 rolls is overpaying, and a trader who offers 30 rolls for a pet that has been selling for 100 rolls is wasting the seller's time. Always research the market value before making an offer.

The second most common mistake is selling a pet in a panic. A trader who needs cash quickly might sell a valuable pet for a discount, not realizing that the pet is worth much more on the open market. Before every sale, check the recent transactions to see what the pet is actually worth, and avoid selling at a discount unless there is a genuine emergency.

The third most common mistake is hoarding pets that are not worth holding. A trader who accumulates petser's goals being sold to fund new opportunities.

## Long-Term Trader Goals

The long-term trader goal is to build a trading portfolio that generates a steady income without requiring active management. The portfolio should be large enough to weather market downturns, diversified enough to avoid catastrophic losses, and liquid enough to take advantage of opportunities.

A reasonable one-year trader goal is to have a portfolio that generates at least 50 percent of the player's rolling budget, and that has appreciated in value by at least 25 percent. This goal is achievable for most dedicated traders, and it provides a strong foundation for the long-term trading playstyle.

The portfolio should also be aligned with the player's overall account goals. A trader who is also a serious pet collector should ensure that their portfolio supports their collection goals, and a trader who is also a serious raider should ensure that their portfolio supports their gear upgrade goals. The best traders are the traders whose portfolio supports their overall account strategy, rather than competing with it.
