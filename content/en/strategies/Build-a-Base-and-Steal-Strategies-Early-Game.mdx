The first week in Build a Base and Steal determines the trajectory of your entire account. Players who make smart decisions in the first seven days build a foundation that supports months of growth, while players who make poor decisions spend weeks recovering from early mistakes. This guide covers the exact priorities for your first week, the common traps to avoid, and the progression milestones that will tell you whether you are on track.

## The First Session: Foundation

The first session of any new Build a Base and Steal account should focus on four things in this order: complete the tutorial, claim your starter rewards, place your first pet, and build your first wall. The tutorial teaches you the basic controls, the starter rewards give you your first batch of pets, the first pet gives you a baseline income, and the first wall protects that income.

Do not spend your first session rolling aggressively, building elaborate bases, or attempting raids. The first session is for learning the interface and the basic mechanics, not for optimization. Players who try to optimize too early usually make decisions based on incomplete information, and they end up rebuilding their accounts within a few days.

The first session should end with a working base that includes at least 4 walls forming an enclosed space, at least 3 pets inside the walls, and a small cash reserve. This minimum viable base is enough to protect your early pets from casual raids, and it gives you a starting point for the rest of the first week.

### Day 2 to 3: Expansion

The second and third days are about expansion, and the focus should be on three priorities: completing the basic wall structure, expanding your pet collection, and upgrading your basic gear. Each priority serves a specific purpose in the early game economy.

The basic wall structure should be expanded to a full perimeter with one controlled entrance. The controlled entrance is the single most important defensive feature, because it allows you to control who enters your base. A base with no entrance is impossible to enter without breaching, which is exactly what you want for early game.

The pet collection should be expanded to 8 to 10 pets, with a focus on completing a common or uncommon family. The family bonus from a complete set is a meaningful income boost, and it is the foundation of your long-term pet strategy. Do not chase rare or epic pets during the first three days; the drop rates are not in your favor, and you cannot afford the investment.

The basic gear should be upgraded to the advanced tier of one category, usually the weapon. The advanced weapon provides a meaningful damage boost and is the foundation of your early game raiding capability. Do not upgrade multiple gear categories at this stage; one strong weapon is better than multiple weak ones.

### Day 4 to 5: First Real Raids

Days 4 and 5 are when you should attempt your first real raids, and the focus should be on safe, low-risk targets. A safe target is one that has weak walls, few active defenders, and a small but valuable pet collection. The goal of the first raids is not to make a profit; it is to learn the raid mechanics and to gain experience.

The best first raid targets are players who are similar to you in progression, who have visible bases with weak defenses, and who do not appear to be online. The risk of being counter-attacked is much lower against an offline target, and the loot from a similar-progression player is usually enough to justify the gear cost.

Each raid should be evaluated after completion, regardless of whether it was a success or a failure. The successful raids should be analyzed to identify what made them successful, and the failed raids should be analyzed to identify what made them fail. This self-review process is the foundation of long-term raiding improvement, and it is more valuable than the loot from any single raid.

### Day 6 to 7: Optimization and Planning

Days 6 and 7 are about optimization and planning, and the focus should be on three things: optimizing your pet collection, optimizing your base defense, and planning your second week.

Optimizing the pet collection means selling or trading away the pets that do not contribute to family bonuses, and focusing on completing the next family in your collection priority. A clean pet collection is more valuable than a large pet collection, and the player who takes the time to optimize during the first week will have a stronger account by the end of the second week.

Optimizing the base defense means adding traps, reinforcing weak walls, and creating a more sophisticated base design. The first week base is a minimum viable base; the second week base should be a proper defensive structure that can resist casual raids.

Planning the second week means setting specific goals for the upcoming week, including pet collection targets, base improvement targets, and raid income targets. Players who set specific goals make more progress than players who play without a plan, and the second week is when the gap between strategic and non-strategic players starts to widen.

### Common Early Game Mistakes

The most common early game mistake is over-rolling. A new player who receives a lucky epic on their third roll often decides to chase another one, and they spend their entire reserve without success. The fix is to set a rolling budget for the first week (usually 30 to 50 percent of available cash) and to stick to it regardless of the outcome.

The second most common early game mistake is under-defending. A new player who focuses entirely on rolling and base building often forgets to add traps, and they end up with a base that looks impressive but is easy to raid. The fix is to add at least one trap for every entrance to your base, and to make sure that the most valuable pets are in the most protected area.

The third most common early game mistake is over-trading. A new player who receives a rare pet from a top family might be tempted to sell it immediately for cash, not realizing that the pet's long-term value is much higher than the cash they would receive. The fix is to research the trading value of every pet before selling, and to keep pets that have long-term collection value.

## Early Game Progression Milestones

By the end of the first week, you should have hit several specific milestones. If you are missing any of these milestones, your progression is behind the typical curve, and you should adjust your strategy.

Milestone 1: At least 10 pets, with at least one complete family bonus. This milestone indicates that you have a working pet collection and a meaningful income boost.

Milestone 2: A full base perimeter with one controlled entrance and at least 3 traps. This milestone indicates that your base is defensible against casual raids.

Milestone 3: At least 5 successful raids, with a positive raid net income. This milestone indicates that you can reliably extract loot from target bases.

Milestone 4: A cash reserve of at least 5,000 cash. This milestone indicates that you have enough cushion to handle emergencies and to take advantage of opportunities.

Milestone 5: An active pet collection plan, with a specific family targeted for the second week. This milestone indicates that you are thinking strategically about your long-term pet collection.

## The Mindset of an Early Game Player

The most important early game mindset is patience. The first week is the foundation, not the payoff. Players who try to rush to the end game during the first week burn out and quit, while players who take the time to build a strong foundation find that the end game comes naturally over the following weeks and months.

The second most important mindset is data-driven decision making. Every decision you make during the first week should be based on the current state of the meta, the current state of the trading market, and the current state of your own account. Players who make decisions based on their feelings, rather than the data, end up with accounts that are misaligned with the actual game state.

The third most important mindset is willingness to learn. The first week is full of mistakes, and the player who learns from their mistakes is the player who improves. The player who repeats the same mistakes is the player who falls behind. Every failure during the first week is an opportunity to learn, and the player who takes advantage of these opportunities is the player who succeeds in the long run.
