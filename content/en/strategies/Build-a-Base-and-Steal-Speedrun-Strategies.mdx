Speedrunning in **Build** a Base and Steal is about efficiency. Every second spent moving, every wall hit, every trap triggered is time that could be spent raiding another base. The fastest raiders complete bases in half the time of average raiders, not because they are faster at clicking, but because they have optimized every aspect of their approach. This guide breaks down the speedrun meta, from route planning to gear selection to the micro-techniques that shave seconds off every wall.

## The Speedrun Mindset

The speedrun mindset is about prioritizing time over everything else. In a normal raid, you might pause to evaluate the base layout, check for traps, or heal after taking damage. In a speedrun, all of those are wasted seconds. You memorize the base layout, you pre-emptively check for traps based on visual cues, and you heal only when absolutely necessary, and even then you heal while moving rather than stopping.

The speedrun mindset also involves a different approach to risk. In a normal raid, you avoid risky moves because a failed raid costs time and resources. In a speedrun, you embrace risky moves if the potential time savings outweigh the risk of failure. A 50-50 chance to skip an entire layer of walls is worth taking if it saves 30 seconds, even if it means you occasionally fail the raid and have to retry.

### The Speedrun Clock

The speedrun clock starts the moment you enter the base and ends the moment you steal the first pet. Everything between those two points counts, including movement, combat, and healing. The clock does not include the time spent in the base selection screen or the time spent preparing your gear, because those are outside of the raid itself.

The optimal speedrun time varies by base type and gear level. A new player with rare gear might complete a simple base in 3-5 minutes. An endgame player with mythic gear might complete the same base in 30-60 seconds. The speedrun meta is constantly pushing these times lower, and the current world records are in the 20-30 second range for the simplest bases and 2-3 minutes for the most complex bases.

## Route Planning: The Speedrun Foundation

### Analyzing the Base Layout
Route planning is the foundation of speedrunning. Before entering a base, you analyze the layout and identify the fastest path to the innermost layer where the valuable pets are located. The fastest path is not always the shortest path in terms of tiles; it is the path that minimizes the number of walls and traps you have to interact with.

A common mistake is to take the direct route through the center of the base. The direct route often goes through the densest cluster of walls and traps, which slows you down. The optimal speedrun route often goes around the perimeter of the base, because the perimeter walls are thinner and there are fewer traps. The route may be longer in terms of tiles, but it is faster in terms of time because you spend less time breaking walls and dodging traps.

### Identifying Trap Clusters
Trap clusters are the biggest time sink in Build a Base and Steal. A cluster of 10 traps can take 30-60 seconds to clear, which is an eternity in a speedrun. The optimal strategy is to identify trap clusters during route planning and avoid them entirely. If you cannot avoid a trap cluster, the next best strategy is to clear it with area-of-effect damage, such as the splash damage from the Epic Sledgehammer or the area-of-effect damage from the Epic Dynamite.

The speedrun meta has identified specific trap cluster patterns that appear frequently in popular bases. Memorizing these patterns and knowing how to counter them efficiently is a key skill. For example, the "checkerboard" pattern alternates traps and empty spaces in a grid, and the optimal counter is to use a weapon with splash damage to hit multiple traps simultaneously. The "funnel" pattern concentrates traps in a narrow corridor, and the optimal counter is to use the Mythic Rope to bypass the corridor entirely by climbing over it.

### The Critical Path Concept
The critical path in Build a Base and Steal speedrunning is the minimum set of walls and traps that must be destroyed to reach the valuable pets. Everything outside of the critical path is optional and should be avoided. The critical path analysis is performed during route planning, and it determines which walls you break and which traps you trigger.

The critical path typically includes the outermost wall of the base, the walls that separate each layer, and the walls that protect the innermost pets. It does not include decorative walls, redundant layers, or walls that protect low-value pets. By focusing exclusively on the critical path, you can complete a raid in half the time of a player who clears the entire base.

## Gear Selection: Speedrun Loadouts

### The Speedrun Hammer
The optimal speedrun hammer is the Mythic Warhammer, if you have access to it. The Mythic Warhammer deals 2000 damage per hit with 50% splash damage, which makes it the fastest wall-breaker in the game. The splash damage also clears adjacent traps, which reduces the time spent on trap clusters.

If you do not have access to the Mythic Warhammer, the Epic Sledgehammer is the next best option. The Epic Sledgehammer deals 500 damage per hit with 25% splash damage. The base damage is lower than the Mythic Warhammer, but the splash damage is still valuable for clearing trap clusters. The Epic Sledgehammer is significantly more accessible than the Mythic Warhammer, and it is the workhorse of the speedrun meta for mid-game players.

### The Speedrun Pickaxe
The optimal speedrun pickaxe is the Mythic Pickaxe, if you have access to it. The Mythic Pickaxe deals 1000 damage per hit with a stacking damage bonus that increases by 20% per hit on the same wall. The stacking bonus makes the pickaxe exponentially faster against thick walls, which is valuable for speedruns because you often need to break multiple layers of walls in rapid succession.

If you do not have access to the Mythic Pickaxe, the Epic Pickaxe is the next best option. The Epic Pickaxe deals 400 damage per hit with a stacking damage bonus that increases by 10% per hit. The stacking bonus is lower than the Mythic Pickaxe, but it is still valuable for speedruns because it accelerates wall-breaking as you progress through the base.

### The Speedrun Rope
The optimal speedrun rope is the Mythic Rope, if you have access to it. The Mythic Rope has infinite durability and allows you to climb any surface, including walls and ceilings. The infinite durability is critical for speedruns, because it allows you to use the rope repeatedly without worrying about it breaking. The ability to climb any surface allows you to bypass walls and traps by going over them, which is often faster than breaking through them.

If you do not have access to the Mythic Raid Rope, the Epic Rope is the next best option. The Epic Rope has 2x the durability of the Rare Rope, which is sufficient for most speedruns. The Epic Rope also reduces fall damage by 50%, which allows you to take risky shortcuts through multi-floor bases.

## Micro-Techniques: Shaving Seconds

### The Pre-Swing Technique
The pre-swing technique is a micro-optimization that reduces the time between attacks. Instead of waiting until your character is in position to swing, you start the swing animation slightly before you reach the target wall. The swing animation is canceled when you change direction, so you can swing early, then immediately move toward the wall, and the swing will connect as soon as you arrive.

The pre-swing technique saves roughly 0.2-0.3 seconds per attack, which adds up over the course of a raid. A typical raid involves 50-100 attacks, so the pre-swing technique can save 10-30 seconds in total. The technique is difficult to master, but it is essential for competitive speedrunning.

### The Movement-Cancel Technique
The movement-cancel technique is another micro-optimization that reduces the time betwen attacks. Instead of waiting for the attack animation to complete before moving, you cancel the animation by moving as soon as the damage is dealt. This allows you to move toward the next wall while the attack animation would normally be playing.

The movement-cancel technique saves roughly 0.1-0.2 seconds per attack, which adds up to 5-20 seconds in a typical raid. The technique is easier to master than the pre-swing technique, and it provides a significant time savings even for casual speedrunners.

### The Healing-While-Moving Technique
The healing-while-moving technique reduces the time lost to healing. Instead of stopping to use a medkit, you use the medkit while moving toward the next wall. The medkit animation plays while you move, which means you do not lose any time to healing.

The healing-while-moving technique saves roughly 2-5 seconds per heal, which adds up to 10-30 seconds in a raid that requires multiple heals. The technique is straightforward to master, and it is essential for speedruns on bases with heavy trap damage.

## Speedrun Practice and Improvement

### Timing Your Runs
The first step to improving your speedrun times is to consistently time your runs. Use a stopwatch or the in-game timer to track how long each raid takes, and record the times for each base type. This allows you to identify which base types are slowing you down and focus your practice on those bases.

The speedrun community has established benchmark times for each base type and each gear level. Compare your times to these benchmarks to see where you stand. If you are consistently slower than the benchmark, analyze your run to identify where you are losing time.

### Analyzing Your Runs
After each run, review what went wrong and what went right. Did you take a suboptimal route? Did you miss a pre-swing? Did you stop to heal when you could have healed while moving? Identifying your mistakes is the key to improvement, because you cannot fix a mistake you do not know you are making.

The speedrun community also shares recordings of record runs. Watching these recordings can help you identify techniques and routes that you had not considered. The speedrun meta is constantly evolving, and staying up to date with the latest techniques is essential for competitive speedrunning.

### The Speedrun Leaderboard
The Build a Base and Steal speedrun leaderboard tracks the fastest times for each base type and each gear level. The leaderboard is competitive, and the top spots are held by players who have mastered every aspect of speedrunning. Climbing the leaderboard is a long-term goal for dedicated speedrunners, and it requires months of practice and optimization.

The leaderboard is also a useful resource for benchmark times. If you are not sure what a good time is for a particular base, check the leaderboard for the top times. Your goal should be to reach the top 10% of the leaderboard for your gear level, which is the threshold for competitive speedrunning.

## Speedrun Etiquette and Ethics

### Respecting Other Players
Speedrunning is a competitive activity, but it should still be respectful. Do not speedrun bases that are clearly designed for new players, because this discourages them from continuing to play. Do not exploit bugs or glitches that give you an unfair advantage, because this ruins the experience for other players. Do not harass or taunt other players about their times, because this creates a toxic community.

The speedrun community has established a code of ethics that all players should follow. The code emphasizes respect, fair play, and community building. Violating the code can result in being banned from speedrun events or from the speedrun community altogether.

### Reporting Bugs and Glitches
Bugs and glitches are an inevitable part of any complex game, and Build a Base and Steal is no exception. If you discover a bug or glitch that can be exploited for speedrunning, the ethical choice is to report it to the developers rather than exploiting it. Reporting bugs helps the developers fix them, which improves the game for everyone.

The developers occasionally host speedrun events where specific bugs or glitches are allowed, but these events are explicitly labeled as such. Outside of these events, exploiting bugs or glitches is considered cheating and is not tolerated in the speedrun community.
